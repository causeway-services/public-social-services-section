I'm humbled to share that someone has provided the funds necessary for my family to purchase land in Minnesota's North Shore, off the coast of Lake Superior. This means we now have a stable foundation to build on. I want to thank y'all for every bit of encouragement, trust, and support you've offered over the years, which has helped us prepare for this shift.

This marks a major change in material position for me and my family. But let me make it clear: just because we have a deed, does not mean the land is ours. Like always, it is its own. The deed is a means to form relations more directly with it, unmediated by the systems and people that prioritized ownership over care. We no longer have to convince anyone of the merit of our ideas: experience has shown us that our non-human kin already understand.

Now, with this land base, we can focus on the work we truly care about: forming and modeling reciprocity with our Earth.  

As we step into this first year, our focus will be on listening and learning. We need to come to know the bioregion intimately—its rhythms, its soil, its waters, its beings—and let that knowledge shape our relationship with the land. We need to heal, too. The trauma of displacement, betrayal, and constant struggle doesn’t leave us overnight, and this land offers a space to process, to breathe, to re-root ourselves.

This shift brings a lot with it: excitement, nervousness, responsibility. Holding land changes the shape of our lives and, inevitably, the ways our values will play out. Our beliefs - our anarchism, our antagonism of settlerism, our understanding of Collapse - are both a lens and a challenge.

It’s one thing to hold these convictions from the margins; it’s another to see how they manifest when you’re suddenly in a position of relative stability. Living as we believe isn’t about perfection; it’s about practice. We’ll get it right, and we’ll get it wrong - sometimes all at once.

What matters is the commitment: to show up, to pay attention, and to keep moving toward relationships that nourish rather than extract, that care rather than control. And we'll be relying on y'all's continued support to hold us accountable when we need it, and to help us meet the needs of the land when it needs it.

This land isn’t a possession; it’s a responsibility and obligation. One we hope to meet, using the tools we've gained through every lesson, every struggle, and every bit of support that's come so far.
