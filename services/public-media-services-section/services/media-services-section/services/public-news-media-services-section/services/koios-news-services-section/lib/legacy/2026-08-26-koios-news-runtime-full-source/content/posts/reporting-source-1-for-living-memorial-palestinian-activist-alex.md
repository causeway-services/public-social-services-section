A participatory art project centered on a public sculpture of Alex Odeh, the Palestinian-American civil rights advocate assassinated in 1985, is transforming a decades-old monument into what organizers describe as a decentralized, community-driven memorial, according to [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/a-living-memorial-to-palestinian-activist-alex-odeh-takes-flight/).

A bronze statue of Odeh stands outside a public library in Santa Ana, California. The figure was created in 1994 by Algerian-American artist Khalil Bendib and depicts Odeh with a book in one hand and a dove in the other — imagery drawn from his role as a peace activist, Hyperallergic reports. The sculpture has been targeted repeatedly since its installation: the dove was stolen in 1996 and again in 1997, the statue was splashed with red paint, and the dove was recovered and reattached as recently as 2020. Brick remnants from a more recent act of vandalism were found scattered nearby, Hyperallergic reports.

Odeh was born in 1944 in Jifna, Palestine, into a Catholic family, according to Hyperallergic. He experienced the Nakba as a child and later lived under the Israeli military occupation of the West Bank that began in 1967. He immigrated to the United States in 1972, earned a master's degree, and taught Arabic at California State University, Fullerton, Hyperallergic reports. By the early 1980s he had become the West Coast regional director of the American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee, where he worked against anti-Arab hate crimes, stereotypes, and discrimination. Hyperallergic describes him as deeply committed to promoting peace and inter-community dialogue among Jews, Muslims, and Christians.

On October 11, 1985, a pipe bomb exploded at the ADC's Santa Ana offices, killing Odeh. He had been scheduled to speak that same day at a local synagogue during Friday prayer services, Hyperallergic reports. No one has ever been arrested for the killing. Investigative journalists and the FBI have identified members of a right-wing Jewish terrorist group as possibly responsible for the bombing, but the case has never been officially resolved, according to Hyperallergic.

Artist Jon Rubin first encountered the statue during a spring 2023 residency at the Grand Central Art Center in Santa Ana, Hyperallergic reports. He told the outlet that a combination of factors drew him into deeper engagement with the work. Many people he met who had come across the memorial were unfamiliar with Odeh's life — and unaware of the violence directed at it.

The project Rubin developed envisions the dove not as a fixed object but as something in circulation. Rather than a statue anchoring a single public square, the aim is a decentralized gathering built around Odeh's legacy through hospitality and shared conversation, Rubin explained to Hyperallergic. The dove was removed from the statue in December; over the following months it is planned to travel to various individuals, organizations, dinners, and community performances as part of the ongoing project.

A documentary about Odeh and the sculpture's history made its debut at the Sundance Film Festival in January, according to Hyperallergic. His murder officially remains unsolved.
