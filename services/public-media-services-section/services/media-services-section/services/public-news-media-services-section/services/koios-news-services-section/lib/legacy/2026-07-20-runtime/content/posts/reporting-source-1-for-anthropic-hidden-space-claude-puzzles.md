Anthropic researchers have identified a specific internal region within their Claude models—a "hidden space"—where the AI processes and puzzles over concepts before generating a final response [Anthropic found a hidden space where Claude puzzles over concepts](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/09/1140293/anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts/). This technique, which the firm describes as a glimpse into what occurs inside its large models, offers a way to monitor how these systems connect phrases and words [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:1, 4, 7].

The discovery concerns the company's flagship LLM, originally released in February, and involves mapping how internal layers represent information during the generation process [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:3, 15]. Researchers suggest that what a model actually "speaks" might differ from the concepts it is internally analyzing within these layers [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:5, 6].

## Mapping the interior layers

The research involves pushing deeper into the internal workings of LLMs to better understand how they carry out complex tasks [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:1, 11]. By observing the "stack" of layers, scientists can isolate how information is passed through the model from input to output [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:14, 15]. 

Much of the heavy lifting occurs in the middle layers, where the model churns through math and complex prompts to prepare its responses [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:18]. While the top layers are often focused on the final production of text, the middle layers appear to hold the internal state where the model puzzles over concepts in a way that is distinct from its final output [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:16, 17, 19].

## Tools for transparency

To facilitate this analysis, Anthropic has shared a demo through the platform Neuronpedia, which allows users to poke inside these models [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:8, 9]. Tom McGrath, a research scientist and cofounder of the startup Goodfire, notes that these tools build an interesting way to understand the internal control mechanisms of LLMs [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:10]. 

The technique, which researchers call an adapted tool, uses existing internal layers to expose deeper information that has not been clearly seen in previous models [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:12, 20]. This research represents an ongoing effort to bridge the gap between the mundane output of an LLM and the unnerving, complex processing that happens within its architecture [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:2, 19]. As interpretability research evolves, the ability to peer into these hidden spaces might change how we evaluate the reliability and behavior of large-scale computational systems [claim:anthropic-found-a-hidden-space-where-claude-puzzles-over-concepts:11].
