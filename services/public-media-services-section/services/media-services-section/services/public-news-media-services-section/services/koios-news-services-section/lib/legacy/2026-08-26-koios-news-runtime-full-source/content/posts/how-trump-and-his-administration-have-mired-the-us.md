In the United States, the federal government is currently grappling with a convergence of public health, environmental, and institutional disruptions as the Trump administration narrows the scope of federal oversight. According to [How Trump and his administration have mired the US in multiple crises](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/19/how-trump-and-his-administration-have-mired-the-us-in-multiple-crises), the administration has simultaneously reduced workforce levels across agencies and rolled back established environmental and scientific research policies. This shift in federal posture has coincided with a nationwide outbreak of cyclosporiasis, a parasitic illness, and ongoing concerns regarding the integrity of election administration.

## Institutional Contraction and Election Oversight

The administration has pursued a significant downsizing of agencies responsible for safeguarding democratic processes. While Donald Trump has frequently questioned the integrity of US elections, his administration has implemented measures that reduce the capacity of federal bodies tasked with defending those same systems. Senator Mark Warner has noted that staffing cuts within the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency resulted in the loss of a third of its workforce, and the administration has proposed a budget reduction of $707m for the agency, citing concerns over waste.

Further institutional changes include the removal of federal commissioners tasked with assisting states in election administration. Within the intelligence community, the appointment of Bill Pulte as acting director of national intelligence was followed by the dismissal of dozens of staff members, according to CNN. The broader federal workforce has also experienced substantial turnover; the FBI has seen a reduction of as many as 2,800 agents since January 2025.

## Public Health and Resource Allocation

Reductions in federal funding have affected the government's ability to track and respond to infectious disease outbreaks. Public health officials report that surveillance of the current cyclosporiasis outbreak—which has resulted in cases of “explosive” diarrhea across five states—has been hampered by a lack of resources. A year prior to this outbreak, the administration cut $11.4bn in funding from state and local health departments and narrowed the remit of programs focused on foodborne illness.

Similar administrative hurdles have affected efforts to manage the spread of the new world screwworm. Former officials indicate that the USDA delayed critical research and construction projects intended to mitigate the parasite’s impact on cattle, including the stalling of a $100m research initiative. The compounding effect of these funding reviews and personnel shifts suggests a weakened capacity for federal agencies to address emerging biological threats, leaving local authorities to manage the consequences of reduced oversight.

## Environmental Policy and Regulatory Shift

Climate and environmental protection frameworks have undergone significant revision, with the administration repealing the 2009 endangerment finding, a regulation that served as the foundation for federal climate policy. The United States has also withdrawn from the United Nations’ main climate treaty and relevant scientific advisory bodies. Officials have closed climate and weather research offices, a move that limits the state's capability to monitor environmental risks such as those posed by wildfire smoke. By prioritizing the expansion of coal and other fossil fuels while simultaneously curtailing renewable energy support, these policy decisions have altered the national trajectory for greenhouse gas emissions, potentially increasing the vulnerability of domestic regions to future environmental disasters.
