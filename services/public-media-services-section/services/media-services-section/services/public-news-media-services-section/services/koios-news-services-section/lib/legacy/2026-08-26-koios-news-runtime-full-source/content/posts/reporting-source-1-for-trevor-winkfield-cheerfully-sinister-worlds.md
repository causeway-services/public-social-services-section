[Trevor Winkfield's Cheerfully Sinister Worlds](https://hyperallergic.com/trevor-winkfields-cheerfully-sinister-worlds/), published July 17 in Hyperallergic, reviews the exhibition "Trevor Winkfield: The Mermaid's Revenge - Paintings 1991-2001," on view at Tibor de Nagy gallery, 11 Rivington Street, Lower East Side, Manhattan, through July 31. The show gathers eight acrylic-on-linen works from a decade when Winkfield had access to an apartment large enough for him to work at a scale he had not previously been able to reach, with canvases ranging from roughly 35½ by 19½ inches to 45 by 72 inches.

Winkfield, born in England, earned a master's degree from the Royal College of Art in 1967 and moved to New York in 1969. There he published a poetry magazine, Juillard (1968–72), and associated himself with the writers and artists of the New York School. He also worked for Arthur Cohen, writing catalog entries covering Dada and Surrealism books and ephemera. In 1976 Winkfield resumed painting, made book covers, and collaborated with poets including John Ashbery, Charles North, Ron Padgett, and Harry Matthews. The following year he mounted his New York debut exhibition and published his translation of Raymond Roussel's *How I Wrote Certain of My Books*. Roussel, according to Hyperallergic, is widely considered the most eccentric writer of the twentieth century and composed using linguistic rules based on homophones. Critics have tried to connect Winkfield's paintings with Roussel's novels without, in the Hyperallergic reviewer's view, finding a key that was ever actually there.

## The Paintings

Hyperallergic describes the eight works as packed with details executed in flat, saturated, playroom colors, interrupted by volumetric passages rendered in a straightforward, illustrative style. The reviewer characterizes the world Winkfield has conjured as simultaneously brightly colored, slightly sinister, and cheerfully incomprehensible, typically inhabited by a delicately featured young man engaged in a mysterious activity. Titles in the show underscore the solitary nature of each scene: "The Student" (1999), "The Navigator" (2000), "The Astrologer" (1991), and "Self-Portrait" (2001).

In "The Student," Hyperallergic notes, a young man wearing a helmet that includes a fish and what may be a lock for an eyepiece holds a long-handled pot beneath a lightbulb releasing tokens marked by a snowflake pattern. Those tokens reappear in the sky above a pink and pale-blue mountain visible through an open window. The figure's body is composed of separate parts, with oversized hands and bare feet painted in colors that include celadon and orange — a combination the reviewer compares to an intricate shadow puppet engaged in an activity whose meaning resists unraveling.

## Writing and Criticism

Alongside his painting practice, Winkfield has produced art criticism, writing on Georges Braque, Jasper Johns, John Graham, and Florine Stettheimer — work the Hyperallergic reviewer describes as smart and original — gathered in *Georges Braque and Others: The Selected Art Writings of Trevor Winkfield, 1990–2009*, published in 2014.

"Trevor Winkfield: The Mermaid's Revenge - Paintings 1991-2001" continues at Tibor de Nagy gallery (11 Rivington Street, Lower East Side, Manhattan) through July 31.
