The Namaygoosisagagun (Collins) First Nation, located about 125 miles north of Thunder Bay and inaccessible by road, was largely destroyed by wildfire in under an hour on Wednesday. Community members went door-to-door to warn their neighbors, and more than two dozen people fled by boat with minutes to spare before the flames overtook their homes. Thirteen Nations are now under evacuation orders that have forced 2,182 people from their homes — a crisis [reported by Grist](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/) that falls with disproportionate weight on First Nations communities. At the center of Namaygoosisagagun's situation is a legal dispute: because the federal government does not recognize it as a First Nation under the Indian Act, the community may be denied the emergency support that recognized communities can access.

As of Friday, [903 wildfires were burning across Canada](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/), 750 of them out of control, with 43 having started within the prior 24 hours. The effect has been greatest in Ontario, where seven First Nations have fled the 193 fires now burning there. According to the Chiefs of Ontario, several communities were cleared under mandatory evacuation orders issued by their own leadership with support from the Ontario Provincial Police. In addition to Namaygoosisagagun, Whitesand First Nation, Kiashke Zaaging Anishinaabek, and Animbiigoo Zaagi'igan Anishinaabek are among those affected by those orders. Dozens of non-Indigenous communities throughout the province have also been ordered to evacuate.

Anishinabek Nation Grand Council Chief Linda Debassige confirmed in a statement that all Namaygoosisagagun community members have been accounted for, pending a full assessment of the damage. "It is with heartfelt sadness that I share that our member First Nation, Namaygoosisagagun First Nation, has been devastated by an unexpected and fast-moving wildfire that advanced toward and through the community, causing extensive damage and destruction to homes and community buildings," [Debassige said](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/). Debassige told The Canadian Press that residents did not receive advance warning from provincial or national officials, nor did they receive evacuation support from outside — neighbors warned neighbors themselves.

The recognition gap that puts Namaygoosisagagun in legal limbo is specific: although its residents are recognized as First Nations people under the Indian Act, the community itself is not. That distinction governs eligibility for federally funded emergency, recovery, and reconstruction support. Meaghan Daniel, a lawyer who represents Collins First Nation, sent a letter to Mandy Gull-Masty, the Indigenous Services minister, calling on the agency to provide the assistance that recognized communities are entitled to receive. "To be clear, if Namaygoosisagagun is denied access to the emergency and rebuilding supports available to recognized First Nations, it will not simply suffer a slower recovery. It may lose forever the opportunity to rebuild the community its members spent decades creating," [the letter states, as reported by The Canadian Press](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/).

In a statement emailed to The Canadian Press, Eric Head, a spokesperson for the Indigenous Services agency, said that efforts are now underway to determine the community's immediate needs and coordinate support to meet them.

Frustration at the pace and structure of the emergency response has been widespread among First Nations leadership. Ontario Chiefs held an emergency meeting Wednesday and expressed frustration with the bureaucratic processes required to access emergency response services, as well as a lack of communication from government officials. Ontario Regional Chief Abram Benedict was direct. "Jurisdictional disputes and administrative delays have immediate consequences and put lives at risk," [Benedict said](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/). "The priority of our leadership is to get people out as quickly and safely as possible. This needs to be everyone's priority rather than financial discussions and technicalities." Benedict added that people are nonetheless coming together to support one another through the crisis.

On Thursday, during its national assembly in Ottawa, the Assembly of First Nations passed emergency resolutions calling for federal support for Namaygoosisagagun and other impacted First Nations, and calling on the federal government to provide ongoing support throughout the wildfire season. The Anishinabek Nation also issued a statement asking the public to keep the community in their prayers. "We ask that you please keep Chief Paavola, the citizens of Namaygoosisagagun First Nation, and all those affected by these wildfires in your prayers, and give them the privacy, time, and space required as they navigate this devastating situation," [the Nation said](https://grist.org/indigenous/thousands-flee-as-first-nations-bear-the-brunt-of-canadas-wildfires/). "We send our heartfelt condolences, and our thoughts are with Namaygoosisagagun members who have suffered this tremendous loss of their entire community; our Anishinabek Nation family will be with you."

The full scope of the destruction at Namaygoosisagagun remains to be established. A complete community damage assessment is still pending. The unresolved question — one that will determine whether Namaygoosisagagun can rebuild at all — is whether the federal government will extend emergency support to a community its own legal framework does not recognize as a First Nation. In the letter Daniel sent to Minister Gull-Masty, as reported by The Canadian Press: "The question is not merely whether recovery will be delayed. It is whether Namaygoosisagagun will have a future in the place it has called home since time immemorial."
