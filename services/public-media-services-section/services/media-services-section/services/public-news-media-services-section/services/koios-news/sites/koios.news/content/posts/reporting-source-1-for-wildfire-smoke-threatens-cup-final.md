Spain and Argentina are set to meet Sunday in New York City in the biggest soccer game the city has held, an open-air final at a stadium that cannot be closed — and according to [Wildfire smoke threatens the World Cup final. FIFA still doesn't have a plan.](https://grist.org/extreme-weather/wildfire-smoke-threatens-the-world-cup-final-fifa-still-doesnt-have-a-plan/), FIFA appears to have no contingency plan for the wildfire smoke that has been pressing down on the region for days. The sport's governing body had not responded to Grist's request for comment, and its prior extensive statement on extreme-heat protocols — which included mandatory water breaks for players — made no mention of air quality.

## A week of orange skies

Canadian wildfire smoke has blanketed large swaths of the East Coast and Midwest, with dozens of cities among the worst-hit, many facing hazardous air quality in recent days. New Yorkers watched the air turn hazy and orange across the week. On Thursday, the state issued an air quality alert as the index climbed into the unhealthy range, and officials advised people to consider avoiding time spent outdoors. By Friday morning, air quality near the stadium remained unhealthy, with forecasts suggesting conditions might improve by Sunday's kickoff — though smoke patterns could still change.

## Stars on the field, smoke in the air

Lionel Messi and Lamine Yamal are both slated to play in that open-air venue, with no way to close it. The Spanish team practiced outdoors in New Jersey on Thursday despite prevailing air quality, with events continuing as scheduled. Smoke exposure of that kind almost certainly means players and fans among the crowd will experience symptoms including coughing, burning throat, and headache. Dr. Courtney Howard, an emergency physician with the Global Climate and Health Alliance, told the Associated Press that high-level athletes moving at intensity draw air deep into their lungs — making the hazard more acute for the people at the center of the match. The risk extends into the stands as well: smoke is particularly dangerous for sensitive groups, including children, older individuals, and people with asthma and other respiratory conditions.

## The protocol gap

Unlike a number of other leagues, FIFA has not implemented standardized air quality warnings or adjustments triggered when quality metrics cross defined thresholds. Grist's review of FIFA's previous, extensive heat-protocol statement found detailed provisions — including mandatory breaks and hydration requirements — with no mention of air quality contingencies. Neither FIFA nor the New York City Health Department responded to Grist's comment request.

The contrast with domestic competition is measurable. The National Women's Soccer League carries a policy: when the air quality index tops 101 for sensitive groups, additional hydration breaks are added; when it reaches 200, games are cancelled or postponed. A match at Citi Field in New York on Thursday proceeded under that framework. Sunday's final has no equivalent structure in place.
