After seven years during which the original GitHub repository sat frozen at its last commit, NextBSD has returned to potential development—under new management, with a new repository separate from the original, and the same founding purpose: porting Apple's open-source Darwin code into the FreeBSD ecosystem. [The Register's FOSS desk reported the revival on July 18, 2026.](https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/07/18/nextbsd-returns-to-dollop-apple-source-on-freebsd/5273788)

NextBSD was started in 2015 by Jordan Hubbard, a co-founder of FreeBSD. The plan from the beginning was to port components of Apple's Darwin OS to FreeBSD. Darwin is the Unix foundation on which macOS and Apple's other operating systems are built; it is open source, with its code available directly from GitHub. Bridging that codebase into FreeBSD required sustained porting effort—work that exists only as long as someone is actively doing it.

## Joe Maloney Takes Over NextBSD

The developer who revived the project is Joe Maloney, known on GitHub as pkgdemon. Maloney is already recognized in BSD circles: he built the Gershwin desktop environment for GhostBSD, which The Register previously covered. Shortly after that coverage, Maloney approached the NextBSD maintainers and asked if he could take over the project. He had a relatively minor role in the original—his commits are visible in the project's history—and now leads the revived effort.

The new GitHub repository is distinct from the archived original, which remains accessible with its history intact. The revived project also includes a project history page that gives credit where it's due, as The Register describes it.

## What the Revival Picks Up

Two artifacts from the original project document what Maloney is now taking on. First, some of NextBSD's initial goals are explained in a presentation referenced in [The Register's coverage](https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/07/18/nextbsd-returns-to-dollop-apple-source-on-freebsd/5273788) of the revival, giving a record of the scope that Hubbard set out when he founded the project in 2015. Second, Maloney's own commit list in the original repository is publicly accessible—The Register notes that readers can see it directly—and it shows a minor contribution record rather than a core-developer one. That minor record is nonetheless the history that connected Maloney to the project before he approached its maintainers about taking over.

[The Register](https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/07/18/nextbsd-returns-to-dollop-apple-source-on-freebsd/5273788) described NextBSD as one of the most interesting BSD variants of the 2010s and said its FOSS desk was intrigued by the revival.
