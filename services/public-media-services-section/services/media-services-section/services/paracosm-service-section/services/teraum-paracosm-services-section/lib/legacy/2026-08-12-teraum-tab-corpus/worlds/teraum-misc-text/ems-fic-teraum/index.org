** #13: Less Than One :noexport:
*/In which an accountant does their job./*

(Tieran calculates the MEROI of Union extraction sites, and one day, discovers, it is below 1: the Union is spending more to extract energy than it's getting out, across its sites.)

Tieran Hornfester's beard was fifteen years old today. They knew that so precisely because they had started growing it when the promotion to their current position had been finalized, and today, as they sat down at their desk, they found a small squashboard box wrapped in copper-coloured paper sitting on it. Inside the box was a small brass nameplate, which declared in a blocky font: "Tieran Hornfest, Senior MEROI Analist"

The "Senior" was new, as was "Analyst" being misspelled. Tieran wasn't entirely sure why they needed a new nameplate anyway. For several years, they'd been the /only/ MEROI analyst, after public concern about the problem had faded, and other analysts had been moved to figure out the EROI of specific extractions. The presumed obsolesence of Tieran's department led others to question why Tieran even bothered doing the work. They'd jokingly ask in the hallways, "How's the MEROI looking?," and walk on before Tieran could even respond: "down another two-hundredths a point, same as yesterday."

But that had been the answer, consistently, for nearly a year. Every day, Tieran looked at a collection of reports that had collected on his desk overnight. Each report was the accounting for daily operations at one of the many extraction sites the Union bureaucratized. It summed up the cost of running the site, and the value of the energy extracted, and divided them. This got what the analysts called an "Energy Return on Investment." It reduced the gains of an extraction site down to a single number: for a unit of energy put in, how many units of energy came out?

The EROI for an extraction site was different every day, and it was Teiran's job to find the average value of that number, between reports: the mean EROI, or MEROI. At one point, there had been more MEROI Analysts, and it was Tieran's job just to figure out the MEROI for one region. But, other Analysts had retired, or been moved to other departments, until it was just Tieran. At this point, they weren't even sure who to deliver the daily MEROI report to. 

Most other analysts were part of a chain. They received reports, and they generated reports, which they delivered elsewhere. But Tieran's elsewhere had been lost somewhere in the bureaucracy. On most days it didn't matter. But Tieran had to tell /someone/ about today's MEROI.

Tieran reflected on the chain they were a part of. The reports that came to Tieran's desk were collated by clerks elsewhere in the Red Ward, copied from originals by a different set of clerks, produced off originals created by another set of clerks, working the Central Receiver that rose up out of the Ward. Those clerks were transcribing messages blinked in from extraction sites around the world, where they were being transmitted by clerks living at those sites.

At the site, work was being done, food was being eaten, with clerks and analysts managing the whole thing, but all of that was rather incidental. That work, that consumption, had been carefully planned by analysts back in the Red Ward, working just a few buildings away from Tieran.

Those analysts were tasked with looking at parcels of land, figuring out what the EROI would be if an extraction site were set up there. How much would it take to shore up the road, build the camp, feed the workers, and so on? And then, how much would they be able to extract the first year? The first five? Depending on the source, a site might only last five years, but some, like the filter-dams built in the foothills of the Worldkeepers, had been operating for decades. Their EROI was only a few points above even, but that was okay, since it didn't take much to maintain them. Other sites, like the Oceanic pumps, that pushed deep into the layers of biolitter held against the stone basin of the Ocean, churning up a slurry of decomposed organisms and industrial solvents to be pumped to the shore for processing and refinement, would only last for a decade before the Ocean floor was nothing but foreign contaminations eating away at the stone underneath. But in that decade, the pump would pull as much magic out of the world as a dozen filter-dams could in a century, and without much more effort.

Different sites, different reports, different numbers, but the same system between them all, and at that scale, after so many years of doing it, the analysts had gotten pretty good at their particular sort of analysis. And Tieran could see how it all led to them, sitting at their desk, figuring out the mean energy return on investment. But they couldn't see where it was meant to go from there.

That kind of analysis had become the open secret that determined the course of the society Tieran was living in. After camps were built, merchants moved closer. More housing was built. Soon, an extraction site was a village. Even before the camps were built, there was a frontier of explorers, land-surveyors, thaumologists[fn:: GNU Terry Pratchett], and the usual assembly of grifters and opportunists that congregate in such frontiers.

No one really discussed how the Union's bureaucratic approach to... life... had become the reason most of them were alive, because there wasn't any space for anything different: with any part and parcel of the land able to be claimed as a resource, everyone knew that anything that didn't belong to the Union, was only free due to temporal circumstance.
