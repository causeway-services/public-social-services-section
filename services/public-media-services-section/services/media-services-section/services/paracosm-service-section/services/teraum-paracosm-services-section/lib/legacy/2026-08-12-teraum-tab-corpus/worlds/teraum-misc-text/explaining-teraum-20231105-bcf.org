#+title: Explaining Teraum, November 5th, 2023

[[id:5b2183de-3e1a-491d-961e-697aa7122907][Teraum]] is a [[id:82586ad4-c546-4681-a097-1f9bdc3efe94][fantasy world]] I've been working on, off-and-on, mostly off, for most of my life at this point. It is, in recent descriptions I've written, called a "tragically funny fantasy world," which I feel is pretty accurate. The world adheres to a lot of the [[id:4fa686a9-c946-4a80-91b3-5181835a778d][tropes]] of fantasy worlds, but... in a different enough way that the world itself feels different, at least to me, from lots of other fantasy worlds.

Explaining any one part of the world doesn't make this clear; it's how the stereotypes and subversions stitch together that I think really make the world. I think I should also note that I have used the world more often as the location for [[id:12bf4375-1392-4d7f-9fa7-5aaf4cb2f739][tabletop role-playing games]] than for [[id:0b1a5a23-d3af-4723-81e9-fff84b55b73e][stories]] I write on my own, so has definitely changed how the world has come to exist.

In my head, it is "currently" [[id:1c12ca46-9f79-411e-871a-9c403a1be0cc][autumn, 82 years]] after what has become known as "the [[id:ceda7c85-a08c-47dc-ba02-de2bd467c1fb][Collapse]]." (It's maybe worth noting that I've been having [[id:dc21f976-8556-48ff-93c3-f99dda48a8a9][human]] of Teraum call their apocalypse a Collapse for about 20 years now - and that what those humans went through was a simplified version of our own ongoing transition into [[id:675fd315-d33e-4c1c-8e69-cb388b477ae9][post-peak-oil]].)

The world of Teraum used to have [[id:d5d38ba9-3d45-44df-aa72-510e60887089][elves]] and [[id:0e99a639-7175-4bd3-af92-1b0864488d31][dwarves]] and stuff: now, there's just humans (more or less). Their society is slowly getting back on its feet, organized by a fascistic bureaucracy known as the [[id:735ff6cc-9733-430d-8975-ea79a3b0acfb][Red Union]], after having fought against a tremendously chaotic world for a few generations.

So, perhaps, a history of Teraum would be appropriate.

There is, for the sake of these stories, a vast [[id:9849f428-15d3-4004-8e62-7077fee1a5ec][multiverse]], in which just about any imaginable thing exists. [[id:b6535bb3-8204-484f-b886-e1ffe729098e][Gods]], in their arrogance, view themselves as exceptionally imaginative, and so imagined a place where they could show off, to each other, their imaginings.

Essentially, a nursery, or zoo, depending on your morality. It was vast, and took quite a lot of work and coordination to accomplish. Hard work and coordination are not things that most gods are keen on, so, the project was slowly abandoned. But not before something, that would eventually become known by a certain group of hominid refugees who were imagined up some anonymous god who took up residence on the mud that accrued around its walls, the [[id:40cbacb0-00fb-4d54-863b-e38004426a58][Worldkeep]]. (Phew, that was a mouthful of a sentence.)

So, again, the gods put some things they imagined together and then got bored, leaving these things on their own. This led to... an incredibly dense and long history, millions of years long, where [[id:7e012907-2273-4c7e-ae69-2615883dcea8][undead]] hordes fought thousand-year wars against [[id:391d7a73-46b0-4f3f-ab06-dac21f3468f8][giant spiders]], while nearby sentient cetaceans composed sonatas.

Most of the Worldkeep existed in deep underground structures - there was a [[id:1cf1036a-c2c7-4494-94d8-a6ca9d7deffb][Surface]] to this world, Earth-like, but it was established more as a botanical garden than any place for the more animate imaginings that had been contributed. (I feel like I briefly had a good writing voice there and then spent it all on that alliterative bit about cetaceans. I can't tell if it is good or not that I have [[id:94e89742-7efc-461e-b795-05c935ce7596][4theWords]] here in the background helping motivate me to just keep throwing words down on the page... it probably is.)

But after a significant length of abandonment, erosion and other natural processes took their toll, and slowly, the Worldkeep began to breach into the world around it.

There led to a [[id:e683a7e6-6eef-413e-97ac-420e18a76bf2][second era]] of history, where the lands above the Worldkeep began to be explored and colonized by the folk who could access it. To make satirizing the Earth easier, the land has roughly two continents, which are known by the humans as the [[id:10d744eb-c618-4137-9d8a-4cec812daf26][Old]] and [[id:d68c7d2c-4dda-4a4e-ba6d-cafcbcb14c1a][New Worlds]]. (Unless otherwise specified, I'm probably going to be using the humans' names for things.)

In the Old World, there were dwarves, and [[id:18dcc94e-7213-46c6-9792-72951399afaa][goblins]], and [[id:6133432f-206f-49d7-92fb-c65e49dbcb48][orcs]], and [[id:f5ddf3e1-a91a-49bd-8654-6626f2aca299][trolls]], and, though they were a bit slow to join, humans. And in the New World, there were elves, [[id:7084d3ee-f9dd-4214-9fc2-c4a81c36d05d][halflings]], and stranger things.

With all these [[id:14527c79-6848-493d-a57b-64439a29edf1][magic creatures]] around, [[id:fbdd3937-d8ca-4559-86dc-9b92425b4f5d][magic]] became so concentrated that over the years, it had became a physical substance, a near-solid sort of gooey substance. In the Worldkeep, it had been collecting for so long that the collapse of various areas had led it to form into something similar to veins of ore, squeezed out and up through the rock toward the surface, where it would later be found, and used, by those settling the surface.

A lot of history happened, that led to there being lots of [[id:705d1dea-f72a-4825-a573-a8815e36c34e][dwarven enclaves]] scattered around the world, but also massive [[id:564a3970-6211-4b14-a9b2-5d32c2de6659][human cities]] built by expert application of magic. There were [[id:1925be9a-f060-447f-880c-f8be2b95887c][war]] through all this, conflicts carried on by creatures who'd been situated too close together in the Worldkeep, but there was also an ever-shifting collaboration between the dwarves, humans, orcs, trolls, and several others.

And then... something happened. Relations between the different sentient species fell apart entirely. The dwarves basically cut off relations with everyone, closing off their enclaves except for highly regulated trade occurring near the entrances. Orcs became outright xenophobic.

And humans... somehow lost how to do magic. It may be that they tried to use magic to do something too big, and in this, broke relations between the species, or maybe they just stopped caring about it as much.

Either way, they stopped being able to do things like shape stone into massive fortress-cities, or summon clouds to carry their goods to market, or water their crops, and became... mundane.

Life for [[id:c43e5834-421c-48fe-9dd6-fc37e5f6c9a6][mundane creatures]] on a magical world is not easy.
