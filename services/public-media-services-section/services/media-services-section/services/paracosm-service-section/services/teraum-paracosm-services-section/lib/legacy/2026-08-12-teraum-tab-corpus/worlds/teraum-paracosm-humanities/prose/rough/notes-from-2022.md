## Tidbits

### Schools

At first, children worked at places like laundromats. With magic
innovations, they had more free time. The Union offered classes in
reading, writing, and arithmetic — to make them more able to participate
in bureaucracy.

### Werewolves

When a widower or widow is alone on a homestead after the departure of
their spouse, badwolves[^1] will start to congregate around it until
the next full moon, when they'll enter the house (somehow) and then the
widower/widow joins their pack.

[^1]: Legit just wolves that don't act like wolves — they'll attack
things that aren't prey, they're cleverer, etc.

### Universities

Universities are where folk who excel at the three R's in school get
sent to learn how to be an analyst or clerk.

### Service contracts and faux fragmentation

In the early days of collapse, most everyone works for the Union, but
as things fall apart, they start awarding service contracts to other
firms — leading to a faux fragmentation, since everyone uses the same
bureaucratic structure.

### Sinus wear

Smelling fizzy blood after smoking a certain herb is an indication of
sinus wear: skip a day of rock processing.

### Robogoat

A person displaced from the city due to resource scarcity ends up in
the savannah.

> "Why don't you farm?"
> "It'd use up all our water to grow one cabbage."
> "Hmm."

The person takes a long-ass time, eating yogurt for breakfast each
morning, designing a machine that only requires more fuel than their
whole village uses in a week to run, but can wander the prairie looking
for fermentable grasses and bushes and harvest them, ferment them in a
container it has, and then provide the resultant protein-rich organism
cluster as food.

> "That's a goat."
> "What?"
> "You're describing a goat. We have one of those. It's where your
> yogurt comes from."
> "Well... more goats?"
> "Not enough grass."
> "More grass?"
> "Not enough water."
> "More water?"
> "Not enough soil."
> "More soil?"
> "More soil."

The person gets taken to help tend to the orchards, which provide
shade and help, over generations, to trap more of the water that comes
in from the seasonal hurricanes.

### Lily the bead sorter

Lily sits at home, sorting through the beaded clothes scrapped by
community members. Folk bring her by what they can, and help bring the
beads to the town center.

### Kids at the exchange

> *kid1:* I can't believe our mom forced us to help here.
> *kid2:* Something about helping the poor being good for our morals.
> *kid3:* Morals.
> *kid2:* Yeah, those.

### Ledgerton (Leisureton, Debton)

Well into energy scarcity, there's a town focused on providing luxury
experiences where an overseer keeps a ledger of everything people use,
points, gotta work to pay off the points. Folk are always growing how
much they're in debt, but being more in debt also privileges you, so
you're inclined to keep trying to pay off the debt rather than leave.

(Combo of Negan's Sanctuary in *The Walking Dead* and any frontier
town.)

You show up under the pretense that you get a few days pampering and
work a week to pay it off, but you're always more in debt — until
eventually you're part of the staff, unless you bring in more folk.

### First grass cutting

The first grass cutting of the year is extra-sweet and precious.

### Magic and the gods

As the magic got extracted, the gods got quieter until they vanished.
As folk learn to see magic in other things, the gods come back.

It starts at the old Chauntean monastery, where Stanley and Holden
curate the *Central Marben Material Exchange*[^2].

[^2]: a.k.a. scrap exchange.

### Time-lagged bubble

Those with money lived in a time-lagged bubble. As collapse unfolded,
they were seen as more and more anachronistic by the adapting
population.

### Bureaucracy/recordkeeping

X wondered if the abundance of bureaucrats, relative to bureaucracy,
was a quirk of living when they did, or if it was going to become a
habitual consequence of how they'd evolved: like nose-picking being the
inevitable, if unsavory, consequence of having evolved dexterous digits
slightly smaller than nose hose.

### "This is the place"

A lot of the folk who first adapt keep trying to find "the place" to
adapt in, before coming to learn there is no place — just, each place
is a Place.

### Colonial comfort

Colonial comfort inhibits love.

### Airships

It wasn't that folk didn't know about the other continent. It's just
that trying to sail there didn't work — the winds always pushed you
back, or the rowers mutinied. Only fueled motors got the job done.
There were air currents that could get you across, but were so slow
without good motors.

### Bottle collectors

Folk collected glass bottles, even before plastic ones became popular.

I had some idea for why that was important, but at the moment I can't
remember. Having a knowledge of glass bottle strengths and weaknesses
would help rekindle glass-bottle making, and the recycling of melted-
down plastics?

### Goblins and elves

If they lived, it was so long ago they are part of the fossils, and
died before humans came to be — at least, in the modern history. (Human
history is likely much longer than what folk say. Most folk say it's
12k years long; other folk say it's 200k.)
