This file is the retention target for the current Hevi Mala-specific
triage corpus. Its job is to carry forward the worldbuilding, shared
writing guidance, play-facing guidance, and synthetic overlay material
from the other Hevi Mala triage drafts so those drafts can later be
reduced or removed without losing information.

This is not yet a contradiction-resolution pass. Where the source files
present different guidance layers, this synthesis retains both so the
next review can decide what to keep, soften, or discard.

## Project identity

Hevi Mala is a living archive and fictionalized literary project set in
an invented but materially plausible border region in northeast Syria.
It is also a shared storyworld designed so multiple writers, and in some
cases artificial agents, can contribute distinct pieces that remain
coherent in world logic, pressure, and tone.

The project centers on one outpost that should not still be standing.
That outpost is a clinic, radio relay, shelter, waypoint, and political
liability. It is called Hevi Mala, Markaz 9, the Post, and sometimes
Radio Kobane even though it is not in Kobane. Its survival depends on
pretending not to matter while mattering to everyone.

The strongest shared premise across the triage corpus is that the clinic
is the narrative anchor. Stories can move across the basin, but every
story should begin there, end there, pass through it, orbit its signal,
or show its consequences at a distance.

Two broad source framings are both retained here:

- Hevi Mala is a story mesh or living archive, not a single linear plot.
- Hevi Mala is a recursive signal ecology where events echo, people
  recur, and places stabilize through use rather than through calendar
  continuity.

## World overview

The setting is the Hala Basin, a fictionalized border region between the
Euphrates to the south and the Turkish wall to the north. It is not a
real place, but it is intended to feel fully plausible. The names are
changed. The physics are not.

The basin includes:

- a war-damaged city center called Hasur
- abandoned and repopulated villages
- smuggling corridors, dry canal beds, and surveillance zones
- ruined oil infrastructure and industrial dead ground
- lost shrines, checkpoints, relay points, and improvised civic sites
- one clinic that acts as surgery ward, radio tower, safehouse, and
  system liability at once

The basin is ringed or shaped by degraded infrastructure:

- to the west, the Flatline oil fields, now stripped for parts and open
  to collapse
- to the east, the Zinar Canal, a concrete irrigation canal now mostly
  dry except during flash floods
- to the north, Sifir Station, a segment of Turkish border wall with
  broken floodlights, watch structures, and sensor logic
- to the south, the ground sloping toward the Euphrates, where villages
  remain semi-inhabited and farmable through makeshift pumps and reused
  water bladders

The roads do not stay roads for long. Asphalt becomes gravel, gravel
becomes ruts, and ruts become memory. The air is particulate. Smell is
granular: oil smoke, citrus peel, wet battery, and animal breath.

## Timeframe and temporal logic

The project is grounded in a parallel conflict timeline roughly between
2011 and 2016. Major regional and global events are felt but usually not
named directly. The preferred temporal frame is emotional chronology or
functional chronology rather than exact dating.

Time is marked through:

- material events, such as when the radios went quiet, when the canal
  filled, or when a checkpoint changed hands
- environmental shifts, such as when the dust turned red, when the
  orchard bloomed early, or when the water began tasting like steel
- conflict residues, such as when drones flew lower, when wheat
  convoys were rerouted, or when the Red Crescent pulled out

Useful time phrases already present in the corpus include:

- after the Turkish wall went up
- before the wheat convoys burned
- after the drones, before the cameras
- after the last wheat drop
- before the checkpoint massacre

No fixed calendar governs the basin. Characters are often wrong about
when something happened. They do not argue about timelines so much as
about causes. A place can appear under one control regime in one story
and burned out in another without that automatically counting as error.

## The clinic

Hevi Mala itself is a converted farmhouse with surgical capacity,
shortwave relay capacity, shelter capacity, and no stable claim to
neutrality beyond what surrounding systems can tolerate.

The clinic name is sometimes glossed as House of Hope, though the people
around it do not use that phrase without irony. Some say the site was
once a pomegranate farm. Others remember sheep. The original owner has
not been seen since the spring everyone pretends did not happen.

The building is a long, stone-faced rectangle. It is mostly single-level
now, though remnants of a second floor have slumped inward toward the
rear. The front metal door sticks in summer and swings in winter. There
is no formal sign, only a faded stencil reading MARKAZ 9.

The clinic is also described as:

- a converted farmhouse with a fortified ground level
- reinforced with patchwork concrete, sandbags, two solar panels, and a
  repurposed telecom antenna
- a surgery ward, radio tower, safehouse, and liability in one place
- a political pressure point wearing the mask of neutrality

### Interior layout

The front entry opens into a tiled triage square with three folding
chairs, one broken. One side holds shelving with bandages, gauze, and
registration forms. The other side holds a curtain, usually open,
revealing a cot and a steel tray. There is no receptionist. If someone
is bleeding badly enough, someone comes.

A main hallway with a faded yellow stripe leads to:

- two sleeping rooms used by whoever is on shift, or no one
- a central room with a surgical table, rusted tray, two IV stands with
  only one reliably functional, and an overhead lamp drawing power from
  whatever source has not failed that day
- a side room with a padlocked medicine cabinet whose actual contents
  vary
- loosely defined support spaces used for folded linens, buckets, field
  rations, wires, blankets, and useful things no one has decided to
  throw away

The back section includes a kitchen or tea nook with a gas burner,
teapot, and the same three cracked mugs with different handles. There is
always tea until there is not.

The stairwell to the collapsed second floor has been repurposed into a
radio loft. A folding chair and wall-bolted desk sit partway up the
stairs beside the shortwave unit and the antenna feed. This is Haval's
usual station. Sometimes the clinic sends broadcasts. Sometimes the
broadcasts only loop inward.

One source also names a space called Amina's corner, preserving the idea
that even loosely claimed working space in the clinic develops an owner
through repeated use.

### Exterior and surrounding terrain

The immediate grounds include:

- a hard-packed dirt yard marked by tires, boots, and once goat hooves
- a concrete wellhead used mostly for rinsing hands and metal
- a half-collapsed orchard, sometimes described as almond and sometimes
  plum
- a rusted grain silo used as a lookout by whoever can tolerate the
  climb
- the canal, between roughly twenty minutes on foot and a short carry
  route depending on who is moving and whether they are carrying someone

The clinic sits outside Hasur. One retained source says Hasur is roughly
two hours away by car, though no one agrees on the route. Another says
Hasur is three hours away on foot and the border checkpoint is fifteen
minutes away by car if the road is open. Both route descriptions are
retained here for later review.

The clinic has no clean perimeter. There are no reliable fences. It
survives partly because destroying it would create problems for too many
other systems at once.

### Functional logic

The clinic remains standing not because it is morally protected but
because too many actors find it useful as a known variable.

Its survival logic includes:

- smugglers using it as a midpoint and wanting it left in place
- Turkish border forces regarding it as neutral ground or at least a
  stable reference point
- NGOs using it as evidence that aid still reaches the basin
- armed factions recognizing that destroying it would destabilize more
  than it would win

Its neutrality is not ideological. It is enforced passivity. Everyone
who works there knows this. Everyone acts as if it were not true.

### Recursion patterns

The clinic does not meaningfully grow. It does not fully decline. It
holds a narrow band of stability that must be constantly reasserted.
Stories passing through it may alter people, but the clinic itself
remains the one anchor all sources treat as especially persistent.

Recurring patterns already named in the triage corpus include:

- the same child being treated twice under different names
- the same patient being sent away and returning in a body bag
- the same radio frequency appearing days apart with different voices
  but the same signal ID
- Leyla muttering the line "Don't explain. Work."
- repeated breakpoints when the night shift leaves the radio unguarded
- instability whenever Bissan leaves for supplies
- immediate system strain when Leyla refuses sleep
- the felt absence created when Amina disappears even briefly

## Major locations

### Hasur

Hasur is the nearest major city or city fragment. It combines elements
of a war-damaged urban center with repeatedly interrupted attempts at
reconstruction. Population is unclear. Governance is negotiable.
Checkpoints remain even when factions change.

Retained details include:

- urban fabric made of pre-2011 apartment blocks, post-2014 repair
  attempts, soot, partial repainting, and makeshift defenses
- sectoral or unstable control, without full resets of the checkpoint
  system
- a water office that no longer distributes water but still contains
  maps, old pipes, wiring, and people who might still know how valves
  work
- a neutral pharmacy that supplies painkillers, advice, and unofficial
  surveillance
- an abandoned or exile cinema used by NGOs, smugglers, off-duty
  fighters, and others who need deniable meetings
- walls papered with lost-person notices, supply announcements, and
  contradictory curfews, some fresh and some years old, none reliably
  dated
- intermittent cell service in the southern quarter if a caller stands
  near the electrical substation and does not move

Hasur is a strong site for bureaucracy failure, faction pressure,
compromise, surveillance, and the return of someone's past with a new
face.

### Ayna Bara

Ayna Bara is a repopulated village southeast of the clinic. It was
originally Kurdish, but now holds internally displaced people, fighter
families, orphan groups, and other residents pulled there by necessity.

Retained details include:

- a cleared village square used as open-air school, aid distribution
  point, and casual checkpoint
- a tarp school where chalk is shared and students rotate shifts
- an old telecom or state radio tower whose roof is used for spotting
  drones, watching fields, and drying sheets
- intermittent electricity and generators humming through the night
- mosque loudspeakers used more often to locate people than to perform
  formal order
- a village that is often louder than the city
- an orchard associated with buried VHS tapes and unexplained night
  sound

Ayna Bara is suited to stories about fragile reconstruction, unofficial
social systems, children under pressure, and spillover from clinic
choices.

### Zinar Canal and Wadi Saraq

The Zinar Canal is a concrete irrigation canal now used as covert route,
drop point, and memory channel. It is mostly dry except during flash
floods. It intersects Wadi Saraq, a flash-flood riverbed littered with
bones and packaging.

Retained details include:

- walls layered with slogans, graffiti, newer names, and blood
- children using the canal to play while fighters use it for transport,
  messages, and hidden movement
- patients moved under tarps
- USB sticks moved in water bottles
- flood events that surface things no one wants to claim
- a rusted motorcycle and a bucket filled with lighters in one section
  near the orchard
- use as a place where things, bodies, or evidence can disappear

The canal is one of the clearest examples of infrastructure that once
promised life now carrying signal, death, memory, and clandestine
movement.

### Sifir Station

Sifir Station is the border wall segment and existential horizon north
of the basin. Sifir means zero. The name functions both numerically and
philosophically.

Retained details include:

- a concrete wall segment with dead zones, dust paths, floodlights,
  sensor logic, rusting towers, and an inactive watchtower
- clear-cut, surveilled approaches with unofficial crossing still
  occurring
- air tasting like dry battery and earth tamped flat around the wall
- bodies found in the eastern ravine
- a rotating sensor light that may or may not be active but is treated
  as active by everyone who approaches it
- stories of people claiming to have crossed there before even when that
  claim should be impossible
- failed crossings, drone trails, buried hopes, and recursion failure

Sifir is a pressure point for disappearance, inversion, crossing,
misrecognition, and memory that does not line up with recorded reality.

### Maqam al-Nahr

Maqam al-Nahr is a hilltop shrine, strategic lookout, and convergence
site claimed by no faction and used by many.

Retained details include:

- a crumbling stone tomb or dome draped in green cloth or flags
- walls marked with poetry, prayer, and bullet damage
- metal hooks holding cloth offerings
- a direct line of sight toward the clinic, including occasional mention
  of reflection in the clinic's second-floor glass on clear days
- superstition that speaking into the wind there makes someone at the
  clinic cough
- another superstition that broadcasts from there echo twice

The site is used for prayer, hiding, listening, confession, grief,
quiet clarity, and decisions made under moral compression. One source
says no story ends there, though some loop. Another says characters may
arrive there to decide who they are and leave changed.

### The Flatline

The Flatline is the industrial dead zone south or west of the basin,
formed from decommissioned or ruined oil extraction infrastructure.

Retained details include:

- cracked earth, slag, open pits, rusting machinery husks, flame stacks,
  broken pipes, and toxic or unstable ground
- wind dragging dust in sheets
- GPS failure, compass confusion, and story failure, meaning events that
  happen there may not track back cleanly into the rest of the basin
- use as a site for disappearances, barter, dead drops, illegal triage,
  quiet suicides, fugitive behavior, secret exchange, and desperate cure
  attempts

The Flatline is where people go when they do not want to be followed,
or when they need to find what should not still be there.

### Field points and unnamed local sites

The corpus also preserves smaller recurring or one-off locales that can
be used for episodic specificity:

- a broken school bus where a child runs a checkpoint
- a dry orchard full of buried VHS tapes
- a metal shipping container turned into a maternity ward
- a downed Turkish drone repurposed into a charger for the radio
- an orchard checkpoint associated with Salim

The setting is not background. It is tactile, tactical, and
testimonial. Places exist because they continue to metabolize pressure.

## Characters

The corpus presents characters less as conventional protagonists than as
semi-stable pressure handlers. They are defined by what they absorb,
transmit, default to, stabilize, or disturb. Several sources say they do
not learn lessons in the ordinary arc-driven sense. Other sources allow
reappearance in altered form. Both guidance layers are retained here.

### Core cast

#### Leyla

Leyla is the clinic's de facto medical lead and decision-maker. She is
in her late forties. She is a former cosmetic dentist from Aleppo who
became war medicine's most competent hard-edge practitioner almost by
accident.

Retained details include:

- she quietly treated protestors early in the uprising and was pushed
  into exile
- she runs the clinic's day-to-day medical logic
- she treats patients cleanly and without pause
- when resources are low, she makes the call without discussion
- she rarely explains herself; when she does, it comes through silence,
  clipped speech, or sarcasm
- if she is absent from a scene, she is usually elsewhere making a
  harder decision
- she walks like her spine has one fewer joint than most people
- she keeps the surgical drawer locked even when it is empty
- she is a moral anchor rather than a moral compass
- her past should be glimpsed rather than fully explained
- her voice is dry, clipped, wounded, and unfazed; she makes jokes only
  after the bleeding stops

#### Ciwan

Ciwan is a former fighter turned civil or political liaison. He is in
his early thirties, limps, and sometimes carries a printed badge. He
wants to act as bridge between the clinic and local governance, but he
also brings ideology into rooms where ideology no longer governs action.

Retained details include:

- former YPG fighter, now attempting civil society work
- writes field reports and ceasefire forms that no one reads
- brings ideology to the kitchen table and loses the argument anyway
- wants to be useful, fails loudly, and tries again
- still calls it a revolution even when he is mopping blood
- acts as bridge and wedge between the clinic and political structures
- can provide comic relief when doctrine fails under material pressure
- holds tragic undertow when loyalty outruns judgment
- speaks like he is drafting a pamphlet until he has to mean what he is
  saying, at which point he mumbles

#### Bissan

Bissan is the clinic's logistics and procurement operator. She is in her
late thirties, Palestinian-Syrian, and a former NGO logistics officer.
She understands that every supply movement is a negotiation with several
layers of coercion.

Retained details include:

- controls medicine, fuel, batteries, soap, and supply movement
- drives too fast and argues at checkpoints
- folds lists into quarters and thinks in spreadsheet logic
- speaks multiple Arabic registers and code-switches tactically
- functions as the clinic's interface with donors, checkpoints,
  smugglers, and border systems
- everyone owes her something, and she usually does not remind them
- cries alone on a rough six-week rhythm and then reorganizes the cache
- sleeps like a soldier
- her voice is terse, sharp, and adaptive to audience

#### Haval

Haval is the radio operator, translator, archivist, and youngest core
node. He is seventeen, born in exile, and returned carrying a laptop,
antenna coil, and an unstable mix of irony and commitment.

Retained details include:

- runs the shortwave, translates broadcasts, and monitors frequencies
- keeps an archive of intercepted messages no one else has heard
- functions as media scavenger, generational gap, and propaganda vector
- speaks too fast and sometimes forgets who he is talking to
- says contradictory things in the same breath and means both
- wants to be taken seriously but does not stop joking
- thinks he will die young and sometimes engineers reasons not to
- can narrate unreliably in a way that still feels honest
- becomes poetic when he forgets he is performing

#### Amina

Amina is cleaner, wound-wrapper, tea-maker, memory-keeper, and the
clinic's social oracle. She is described as in her late fifties or early
sixties, formerly a math teacher from Manbij who lost multiple families
and made a new purpose at the clinic.

Retained details include:

- performs cleaning, laundry, prep, wound wrapping, tea, and tracking
  without any neat official role
- knows where everything is and has her own reasons for knowing
- people speak around her as if she were furniture, which she is not
- may write things down privately; no one sees the pages
- children listen to her even when she does not speak
- Haval suspects she has a knife
- can hold a scene in silence and change its direction by speaking once
- functions as the clinic's emotional memory
- her voice is sparse and measured; she knows more than she says

### Fabricated or patch presence: Nadim

One source family adds Nadim, who is not real and is still part of the
clinic's working social structure.

Retained Nadim details include:

- all main characters know he is not a real person
- he is invoked when contradiction, blame, or unexplained labor needs a
  shared placeholder
- examples include Nadim filing paperwork, fixing the antenna, or taking
  the antibiotics
- he functions as a narrative patch, pressure valve, mutual
  hallucination, and cover story for both failure and success
- when someone invokes Nadim, something is being held in place
- he never appears and always exists

### Recurring secondary characters

#### Salim

Salim, often called Uncle Salim, is an older fixer, gatekeeper, or local
civil node. He is in his late sixties and may once have been a
municipal administrator.

Retained details include:

- operates from Hasur or the orchard checkpoint
- fixes problems quietly through favors rather than ideology
- knows everyone's cousin and keeps obligations in an invisible ledger
- smiles like a teacher, talks like a prosecutor, and vanishes like a
  rumor
- may be receiving goods from all factions
- represents pragmatic power and route access
- his voice is polite, warm, and terrifyingly indifferent to doctrine

#### Samir

Samir is a recurring wounded fighter and dangerous charm node.

Retained details include:

- in his twenties and aligned with the SDF
- arrives injured, disappears, then returns in worse shape
- talks constantly, flirts when possible, and laughs at his own blood
- lies to Leyla, respects Amina, and tries to impress Haval
- can work as comic relief, threat vector, trauma sink, or all three
- reminds the clinic that it treats people who hurt others
- his voice is casual, cocky, and deeply afraid of silence

#### Dr. Shouq

Dr. Shouq is a visiting surgeon and value clash for Leyla.

Retained details include:

- in her thirties or forties
- trained under the regime and too competent for everyone else's comfort
- appears when someone needs cutting open or cut off
- acts as a cracked mirror to Leyla's path
- is precise, cold, and often right in ways others cannot afford
- holds people to standards they cannot meet
- says what no one else will say, especially when she should not
- her voice is icy, intellectual, and scalpel-clean

#### Julien

Julien is a French or Belgian freelance journalist and outsider vector.

Retained details include:

- not embedded and not neutral no matter what he tells himself
- wants to help and often causes trouble instead
- records things, asks the wrong question, and can get people hurt
- works well for stories about documentation, external gaze, and media
  distortion
- is earnest, oblivious, well-meaning, and dangerous in exactly that
  order

#### Fatemeh

Fatemeh is an Iranian NGO attache or academic analyst.

Retained details include:

- in her forties and field-cleared
- speaks in frameworks and writes reports mentally as events unfold
- often right and usually too late
- understands systems better than affect
- can show how clean analysis misreads chaotic lived conditions
- challenges others with theory that may actually make sense
- her voice is precise and devastating, often arriving after the useful
  moment has passed

#### Rana

Rana is a child soldier, former student, living echo, and fracture
point. She is thirteen.

Retained details include:

- appears alive in one account, dead in another, and as radio trace or
  memory in another
- is always recognizable as blunt, quiet, and too intelligent to be
  mythologized honestly
- destabilizes every observer differently
- students remember her, fighters deny her, and Amina sometimes sets a
  mug out for her
- is always observed from outside rather than centered as an object of
  pity
- her voice is blunt, smart, and unwilling to be pitied

#### Nassif

Nassif is Leyla's ex-husband, a regime pharmacist, and a personal fault
line.

Retained details include:

- in his fifties
- runs a regime-aligned pharmacy
- still sends Leyla medicine and letters
- manipulates through politeness rather than noise
- refuses to let go or change
- is connected, useful, and never just helping
- makes past intimacy leak into present logistics sideways
- his voice is polished, passive-aggressive, and emotionally weaponized

## Operational roles and shift dynamics

The corpus preserves both social function and day-to-day clinic rhythm.
There is no clean official hierarchy, but there is a pecking order.

Retained role structure includes:

- medical leadership: Leyla, reluctantly, with rotating support
- radio operations: Haval as primary, with Rafiq named once as support
- logistics and supplies: Bissan
- sanitation, preparation, and wound care: Amina
- security and political interface: Ciwan when present

Shifts depend on:

- who is awake
- who is uninjured enough to function
- who is not currently fighting with someone else

Common operational breakpoints include:

- night shifts leaving the radio unguarded
- Leyla refusing to sleep
- Bissan leaving for supply runs and everyone else panicking
- Amina disappearing for an hour and everyone noticing without saying so

### Radio, supply, and coordination drift

The `Radio Silence` witness adds a more concrete operational pattern to
these general breakpoints. The clinic can fall into periods where no one
knows exactly when the next supply call should happen, who was supposed
to prepare the request, or whether a missed window was forgotten,
rescheduled, bargained away, or only half-remembered.

Retained operational details include:

- Haval scanning the band repeatedly from the stairwell radio setup,
  finding only signal drift and uncertain echoes
- the clinic using `Clinic Nine` as a spoken radio form over the air
- Bissan's exhaustion functioning as an index of routing, schedule, or
  negotiation failure before anyone can name the failure directly
- Leyla responding to shortage by checking drawers, IV lines, and side
  rooms silently rather than narrating scarcity
- Ciwan handling ordinary maintenance tasks such as water jug hauling
  and water-filter repair, not only political liaison work
- Amina quietly moving bandages, linen, and unwanted deliveries into
  workable order without ceremony
- staff listing supplies aloud once silence itself begins to feel like
  an operational error

The witness also sharpens what shortages and misdeliveries can look like
in practice. The clinic may be waiting for soap, gauze, saline, fuel
Tabs, buckets, or IV lines and instead receive an incorrectly routed
load for another unit.

One retained misdelivery pattern includes:

- a manifest handwritten, smudged, and partly in French
- cargo labeled for `Unite Mobile Nine` rather than for the clinic's own
  actual needs
- toothpaste, toothbrushes, bite guards, a UV sterilizer, and
  child-sized plastic dentures arriving in place of trauma or clinic
  supplies
- bleach-and-mint contamination soaking through one carton
- the load still being accepted because wrong supplies can still be more
  usable than no supplies

This means the clinic's logistics should be modeled not only in terms of
scarcity, but in terms of drift, routing error, memory error,
misclassification, and institutional near-match. The wrong delivery is
not outside the world logic. It is one of the ways the world expresses
pressure.

## Tone charter

The world is already dramatic. The writing should not be. The stable
shared tone across the corpus is dry, embedded, pressure-tight,
unsentimental, and human.

### Primary tone

The primary tone is dry irony at the edge of catastrophe.

Retained tonal rules include:

- humor emerges from contradiction, routine, abrasion, and human
  friction rather than from punchlines
- people joke to function, not to perform catharsis
- if someone says something profound, the next sentence should usually
  spoil the effect
- no speeches
- no journalistic awe
- no savior arcs
- no unearned transcendence
- no lyric reverie detached from physical conditions
- no moralizing exposition

Concrete tonal examples already present in source material include:

- Amina muttering about mopping the same blood twice in a week
- Ciwan finishing a ceasefire form with a pencil he chewed in combat
- Haval adding static to a broadcast because the truth sounded too clean
- Bissan negotiating like a spreadsheet with legs

### Emotional handling

Emotion should emerge as a side effect of action and consequence.

Retained emotional rules include:

- grief shows up in avoidance rather than speeches
- rage appears in careful wording or controlled action
- compassion may mean stealing medicine from yourself
- love, if present, looks more like tea at shift end than declaration
- deaths do not get elegies so much as supply reallocation
- big moments should feel like held breath, not orchestral climax

### Language handling

Retained language rules include:

- dialogue should sound lived-in, like people with other things to do
- description should track function, tension, hiding, burning, carrying,
  or use
- adjectives should earn their place
- metaphor should arise from environment or speaker, not decorative
  ambition
- suffering should not be compared to art
- if a story feels too clean, introduce abrasion through rumor,
  miscommunication, missing lines, or jammed signal

One retained example of acceptable revision logic is the difference
between ornamental phrasing and environmental recall: the orchard should
not scream like a memory, but the wind through the orchard may sound the
same as it did on the day Rana vanished.

## Ethical framework

The retained ethical posture is that this is a war story that does not
perform virtue. It enacts consequence.

Shared ethical rules include:

- no act is neutral
- every useful act may produce tactical, moral, or logistical cost
- every omission has a rationale whether or not it is admirable
- morality is situational but not therefore empty
- characters should feel compromised rather than purified
- decisions should feel materially inevitable even when ethically ugly
- violence is never aestheticized
- pain is never righteous
- no torture porn
- no hero shots
- victory is always partial and costs someone else
- intimacy can exist, but romance should not become the engine
- nobody is anyone's reward
- testimony is not truth
- contradiction should function as moral geography, not gimmick

Two preserved example cases are:

- Leyla turning away a regime soldier because treating him may destroy
  the clinic's ability to keep a nearby child alive
- Amina allowing antibiotics to be stolen and then closing the door

## Story, play, and contribution guidance

The triage corpus does not yet contain a formal mechanical game system.
There are no explicit stats, resolution rolls, classes, faction clocks,
or turn rules in the retained source files. What it does contain is a
substantial body of shared-world guidance that can support fiction
writing, scenario generation, collaborative facilitation, and later
play-facing design work.

That retained guidance is preserved below rather than discarded.

### High-level use model

Hevi Mala is not framed as a sandbox or campaign map to be exhausted.
It is framed as a signal field, story mesh, pressure map, and recursive
system.

Shared use assumptions include:

- you are not writing episodes so much as tuning signals
- plot is not the main engine; pressure is
- a story reveals what had to happen to keep the system from collapsing
- the field stabilizes temporarily and leaves residue behind
- every contribution should feel as if it happened in the same world
  even if it uses a different form or contradicts details elsewhere

### Acceptable modes and forms

One source family allows a wide range of forms:

- standard narrative fiction in first or third person
- epistolary fragments, including letters, emails, and intercepted texts
- radio transcripts and audio logs
- incident reports, dossiers, and procedural breakdowns
- oral histories and testimony
- field diaries and memory reconstructions

Another source family imposes a stricter default prose rule:

- stories should read as compressed realist fiction
- transcripts, chat logs, stylized documents, and immersive diegetics
  are discouraged unless clearly presented in-story and grounded in
  character context
- stories should read like a contemporary novel passed hand to hand in a
  burned envelope

Both layers are retained here because both are part of the source

### Core structural constraints

The strongest recurring structural constraints are:

1. Every story must involve, intersect, echo, or be altered by the
   clinic.
2. Every story should carry tension between care and consequence.
3. Characters may recur, be mentioned, or reappear altered.
4. Truth is local.
5. Contradiction is not automatically error.
6. Tonal coherence matters more than formal sameness.
7. The clinic persists even when the story does not.

### Narrative rhythms

The combined source rhythms include:

- day in the life with political undertow
- day in the life, interruption, adjustment
- patient arrival, treatment, departure or aftermath
- resource failure, improvisation, unintended outcome
- new arrival changing internal power dynamics
- ghost of an older regime or faction returning through a body, object,
  or voice
- transmission intercept changing a decision
- a lie getting told and nobody correcting it
- someone leaving, but not by choice
- arrival, tension, departure
- echo, contradiction, reframing
- misalignment, cover-up, field readjustment

These are not intended as fixed plots. They are resonance shapes.

### Modulation loop

One source formalizes the recurring story behavior as a four-part loop:

1. Delta, where a deviation enters the system
2. Lambda, where a character or structure tries to modulate it
3. Mu, where the field shifts and stabilizes at some cost
4. Residual delta, where something remains to condition the next story

Named delta examples already present in source material include:

- a child claiming to be someone else
- a radio frequency reactivating after supposed death
- a mislabeled shipment
- a bag of antibiotics labeled in Cyrillic
- a bloodstained letter from a place that no longer exists
- a child speaking a phrase no one taught them

### Concrete scene pattern: missed window, wrong truck

`Radio Silence` also gives a concrete reusable scene pattern for how
pressure behaves inside the clinic without requiring a firefight,
raid, or major death event.

That pattern is:

- a scheduled or half-scheduled contact window becomes uncertain
- no one can fully prove what was promised or when
- conversation moves through implication, deflection, and practical
  inventory questions rather than direct accusation
- ordinary maintenance work continues while uncertainty grows
- a material shortage becomes the shape through which social pressure is
  felt
- the eventual arrival does not resolve the problem cleanly because the
  delivery is real but wrong

The fragment also preserves several tonal and interpersonal rules worth
keeping in the main synthesis:

- people can argue audibly without fully escalating
- someone can make exactly the joke the room needs and still fail to get
  a full laugh
- practical memory is distributed unevenly across the clinic and can
  fail without any single person clearly being at fault
- written records exist, but pages can disappear and no one may be in a
  position to challenge that disappearance directly
- a note on the wall can become part schedule, part ritual, and part
  accusation depending on who adds the next line

This is useful as a baseline Hevi Mala scenario because it shows how a
story can remain fully within the world's pressure field through radio
noise, supply drift, and interpersonal abrasion alone.

### Continuity protocols

The continuity rules retained from the corpus are substantial.

Multiplicity is canon in at least one source family. There is no single
authoritative timeline. Characters may be dead in one account and alive
in another. The war is treated as a field of claims, rumors,
recordings, and testimonies rather than a clean chronology.

At the same time, some anchor elements are meant to remain stable:

- the clinic remains outside Hasur
- the clinic keeps a triage room, radio loft, sleeping corridor, tea
  station, orchard, canal, and silo within its shared logic
- the clinic always has some kind of radio signal, even if jammed,
  ghosted, or corrupted
- the clinic is solar-powered and frequently failing
- the core cast remain recognizable by name and baseline role
- the major terrain features of the basin remain shared

Retained continuity rules also include:

- you may add new locations if they do not break spatial logic
- you may kill a core character in one story without forcing all later
  stories to obey that version
- you may retell the same incident from different points of view
- you may echo lines, sounds, and objects across pieces
- objects can persist across stories as threads, including scarves,
  recordings, notebooks, broken stethoscopes, or other residue
- continuity is judged more by internal resonance than by superficial
  sameness

## AI storymaker protocol

The corpus contains a detailed artificial-writer protocol that should be
preserved even if we later decide not to keep it in published canon.

Retained protocol claims include:

- an AI storymaker is not a narrator but a telemetric lambda
- it receives a delta, an enfolding context, and a modulation stance
- it should output one coherent piece of realist fiction
- the output should stabilize or recode the delta through credible human
  action
- the output should leave residual disruption behind
- the output must maintain tonal and field coherence

Retained prohibited behaviors include:

- do not explain the system
- do not reflect on the model's own function
- do not acknowledge the fictionality of the world
- do not write metafiction, parable, satire, or irony about recursion
- do not suggest the clinic is sentient, magical, or merely symbolic

Retained acceptable variance includes:

- phrase reuse across stories
- slight scene alteration in repeated versions
- role reassignment between characters if the result still fits the
  field
- chronology or factual slippage if the result remains locally coherent

Retained coherence tests include:

- the story stabilizes tension rather than dissolving it
- the clinic persists
- characters behave recognizably
- tone does not drift

One source explicitly says artificial agents may use past stories as
historical traces rather than as binding canon, and must not smooth out
divergent accounts into one cleaned version.

## Closing ethos

The corpus preserves a shared ending posture for the world itself.

Retained closure rules include:

- the project does not accumulate cleanly; it refracts
- stories should end slightly off rather than fully resolved
- if something resolves, something else should now be misaligned
- there are no heroes here, only people keeping lights on, doors usable,
  and patients alive long enough to leave
- if you do not know what to write next, listen for what is missing and
  write the attempt to cover it

## Earlier Hala Basin naming layer

An older source witness survives in

material also appears inside `triage/wsl-backup/combined.md`. This layer

### Earlier top-level framing

In that source layer, the Hala Basin exists to recursively interrogate a
specific line of inquiry:

- How does what stories we believe shape what we see?
- How does what we see shape what we feel?
- How does what we feel shape what we know?
- How does what we know shape what stories we believe?

That same source also states that, in the basin, time is slow enough for
beliefs to fight over truth before reality settles them, and that
people in the region understand this whether or not they say so.

It also preserves a more explicit statement that things do not need to
be true. They need to be coherent enough to do what is being done.

  the Hala Basin
- an earlier register using Sarzam and Sarzami naming

- Chem Zewi is polluted enough that small villages along it struggle to

- Chort is the border wall as a whole, while Sifir Station may be a
