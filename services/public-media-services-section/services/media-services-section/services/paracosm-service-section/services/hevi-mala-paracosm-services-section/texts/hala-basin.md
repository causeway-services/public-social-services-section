The Hala Basin is the setting of [Hevi Mala](project-frame.md), a dry
borderland between the Euphrates and a fortified northern wall. It is a
fictional place, but it is written to remain materially plausible. The
roads fail by degrees. Water infrastructure works unevenly. Old
military lines, aid systems, smugglers, and agricultural remnants all
continue to shape what people can do.

Several zones give the basin its pressure. Hasur concentrates urban
ruin, checkpoints, and procedural survival. The Flatline preserves the
wreckage of stripped oil infrastructure. Zinar, a dry canal, turns old
irrigation works into covert passage. Sifir Station marks the wall as a
surveilled edge that still permits rumor, memory, and illicit crossing.
Maqam al-Nahr holds prayer, superstition, and watchfulness above the
basin's ordinary routes.

At the center sits Hevi Mala itself, also called Markaz 9 or the Post.
The site is a clinic, a relay node, and a shelter at once. Its interior
is sparse and functional: a triage space, sleeping rooms, a surgical
room held together by failing equipment, a kitchen nook, and a radio
station improvised into a damaged stairwell. Outside it are a dirt
yard, a near-dry wellhead, a damaged orchard, a rusted silo, and the
walk toward the canal.

The clinic survives less by moral consensus than by distributed
usefulness. Smugglers need it as a midpoint. Militias know its
destruction would create new instability. Border authorities treat it
as a known variable. Aid systems can point to it when they need proof
that some form of care still exists. Its neutrality is operational, not
ideal.

Time in the basin works through relation rather than strict dating.
People remember when the radios went quiet, when the wheat convoys
burned, or when the drones started flying lower. Places recur because
they continue to be used. Stories need not agree on every fact to stay
inside the same world. The basin holds because repeated acts of care,
risk, rumor, and repair keep holding it together.
