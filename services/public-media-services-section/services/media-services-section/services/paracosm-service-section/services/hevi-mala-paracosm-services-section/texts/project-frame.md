Hevi Mala is a fiction project organized around a single durable site:
a clinic and relay outpost in the Hala Basin, a fictionalized border
region modeled on the pressures of post-2011 conflict collapse in
northeast Syria. The clinic is the project's center of gravity. Every
story either passes through it, depends on it, or feels its effects at
a distance.

The project is built as a story mesh rather than a single plot line.
It can hold conventional prose fiction, letters, transcripts, incident
reports, testimony, and other short forms, but those forms must still
read as parts of the same world. Formal variety is allowed. Tonal drift
is not.

The fiction treats care and consequence as inseparable. Medical work,
translation, logistics, shelter, and broadcasting all carry political
cost. No act is neutral. A helpful decision in one scene can expose the
clinic in another. Characters do not function as heroes who rise above
the setting. They function as workers, smugglers, medics, organizers,
and survivors whose decisions keep a narrow band of life intact.

That pressure gives the project its tone. The language should stay
compressed, specific, and observant. Humor belongs in human friction,
not in the war itself. The project rejects heroic framing, spectacle,
and any gaze that treats local endurance as an object of awe.

Continuity in Hevi Mala is relational rather than cinematic. Characters
may recur by rumor, injury, radio traffic, paperwork, or altered
reappearance. Events can contradict one another at the level of report
without breaking the world. Truth is local, and contradiction is often
part of the evidence rather than a sign that the setting failed.

## Project constraints

- Every story intersects with the clinic, even when the clinic remains
  offstage.
- Every story carries tension between care and consequence.
- Every story belongs to the same tonal world, even when its form
  differs.
- Violence is never aestheticized, and victories remain partial.
