The recurring cast of [Hevi Mala](project-frame.md) gives the project
its human scale. These characters are not built as heroic arcs waiting
for resolution. They are pressure-bearing figures whose repeated work,
conflict, habits, and absences keep the clinic legible across stories.

## Core characters

**Leyla** leads the clinic's medical decisions. She is a former dentist
from Aleppo who became a war medic through necessity rather than
calling. In story use, she anchors decisions where care, scarcity, and
risk collide. She should read as dry, clipped, and difficult to move.
Her authority stabilizes the clinic, but it does not simplify the
moral field around it.

**Bissan** handles logistics, procurement, and the practical politics
of movement across checkpoints, donors, and supply chains. She is the
character who makes external systems visible inside the clinic. Stories
that turn on fuel, medicine, paperwork, or access can use her to show
how every resource arrives through negotiation rather than innocence.

**Haval** runs radio operations, translation, and signal work. He is a
young character whose irony, verbosity, and contradictory speech make
him useful for stories about propaganda, intercepted messages,
generational mismatch, and the unstable line between witness and
performance.

**Amina** holds the clinic's memory through ordinary labor: cleaning,
wrapping wounds, making tea, watching, and remembering. She is often
best used as a quiet directional force. A scene can change because she
speaks, because she withholds speech, or because other characters fail
to notice how much she has already understood.

**Ciwan** links the clinic to local political structures. He carries
ideology into a site that cannot afford pure principle. Stories that
need factional pressure, administrative friction, or a failed attempt
at principled order can use him as both bridge and wedge.

## Recurring characters

**Uncle Salim** represents pragmatic civil power. He trades in favors,
routes, and reputations rather than doctrine. He works best in stories
where consequences travel beyond the clinic walls.

**Samir** is a fighter and repeat patient whose talkativeness, injury,
and opportunism keep the front line present inside the clinic. He is a
useful carrier of contradiction because he can be charming, observant,
and dangerous in the same scene.

**Dr. Shouq** brings technical competence and value conflict. She is a
mirror to Leyla, especially in stories that make medical precision and
political compromise collide.

**Julien** functions as an outsider vector. He is useful when a story
needs to show how documentation, foreign attention, or an apparently
helpful external gaze can distort the local logic of survival.

**Fatemeh** brings systems thinking into a world that often exceeds any
clean framework. She is best used when analysis clarifies one part of a
situation while failing to absorb its emotional cost.

**Rana** is a recurring fracture point. She appears as child, soldier,
memory, rumor, or signal, and different characters read her
differently. She should never collapse into a simple symbol of
victimhood.

**Nassif** ties supply, betrayal, and emotional residue together. He is
most useful when the past re-enters the clinic sideways through access,
letters, or dependence.

## Use in the project

These characters do not need to appear together, and they should not be
treated as a fixed ensemble. Each is a reusable anchor for a different
kind of pressure: medical, logistical, political, emotional, or
narrative. Their continuity comes from recurrence and relational
position rather than from a single master plot.
