A home-based business operates from the owner's residence rather than a commercial space. This includes freelancers working from a home office, bakers operating under [cottage food laws](./cottage-food-laws.md), crafters producing goods for online sale, consultants meeting clients virtually, and service providers (tutors, bookkeepers, pet sitters) who manage operations from home.

### Advantages

- **No commercial rent**: The largest fixed cost for most businesses is eliminated. The home office deduction ($5/sq ft, up to $1,500) partially offsets the personal space used.
- **No commute**: Time and money saved on transportation.
- **Low startup capital**: No [build-out](../terms/use-of-funds.md), no [commercial lease](./commercial-lease.md) deposit, no separate utility accounts.
- **Flexibility**: Hours can be adjusted around family obligations, another job, or personal preferences.

### Constraints

- **Zoning**: Many residential zones restrict or prohibit commercial activity. Common restrictions: no customer foot traffic, no employees working on-site, no exterior signage, no commercial vehicles parked at the residence. Check your municipality's zoning ordinances before starting.
- **HOA rules**: Homeowner associations may impose additional restrictions beyond zoning — some prohibit any business use of the property.
- **Insurance**: A standard homeowner's or renter's policy typically does not cover business equipment, inventory, or liability. A business rider or separate [business insurance](./business-insurance.md) policy may be needed.
- **Space limitations**: Production capacity is constrained by the size of the home kitchen, garage, or spare room. Growth beyond a certain point requires transitioning to a commercial space.
- **Professional perception**: Some clients or customers may perceive a home-based business as less established. This matters less in industries where remote work is normal (consulting, design, writing) and more in industries where a physical presence signals legitimacy.

### Tax implications

The home office deduction is available if a portion of the home is used "regularly and exclusively" for business. The simplified method deducts $5 per square foot (up to 300 sq ft = $1,500). The actual expense method deducts the business percentage of mortgage/rent, utilities, insurance, and maintenance — more complex but potentially larger. See [Small Business Tax Strategy](../curricula/small-business-tax-strategy.md).
