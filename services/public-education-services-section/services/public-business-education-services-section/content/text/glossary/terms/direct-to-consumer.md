Direct-to-consumer (DTC) means selling directly to the person who uses the product, with no retailer, distributor, or wholesaler in between. The baker who sells bread at a farmers' market is DTC. The woodworker who sells cutting boards through their own website is DTC. The soap maker who sells at craft fairs and takes orders through Instagram is DTC.

### Why DTC matters for small producers

**Higher margin**: A jar of jam sold wholesale to a grocery store at $4.50 (store retails it at $8.99) earns the producer $4.50. The same jar sold DTC at a farmers' market for $8.00 earns $8.00. The producer captures the full retail margin — minus the cost of the market booth, rather than the wholesale-retail spread.

**Customer relationship**: DTC producers know their customers by name, hear feedback directly, and can adapt in real time. Wholesale producers know their buyer at the store — not the person eating the jam.

**Brand control**: The producer controls how the product is presented, described, and priced. In a retail store, the product sits on a shelf next to competitors, presented however the store chooses.

### DTC channels for small businesses

| Channel | How it works | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Farmers' markets** | Weekly booth rental, face-to-face sales | $20–$75/week booth fee |
| **Craft fairs and pop-ups** | Event-based selling, 1–3 days | $50–$300/event |
| **Own website** | E-commerce storefront (Shopify, Square Online, Etsy) | $0–$40/month + transaction fees |
| **Social media** | Orders through Instagram DMs, Facebook Marketplace | Free (time cost) |
| **Farm stand / roadside** | Physical selling from the property | Minimal (signage) |

### The DTC constraint

DTC requires the producer to do everything: make the product, market it, sell it, package it, handle payments, manage customer service, and fulfill orders. This is manageable at small scale — 50 jars of jam per week. It becomes overwhelming at scale — 500 jars per week — which is why growing DTC businesses eventually add wholesale, hire help, or choose to stay small intentionally.
