A side hustle is work done outside of a primary job to generate additional income. It can be a product (selling baked goods, handmade crafts, screen-printed shirts), a service (tutoring, lawn care, freelance design), or a platform-based activity (selling vintage items on Etsy, driving for a delivery service, renting a spare room).

What distinguishes a side hustle from a hobby is the intent to earn money. What distinguishes it from a full business is that it operates alongside — and is usually subordinate to — a primary source of income and benefits.

### Why side hustles matter in business education

The existing curricula in this series assume a business that is someone's primary enterprise — a restaurant, a retail shop, a service company. But many businesses start as side hustles: a home baker who sells at farmers' markets, a graphic designer who takes freelance clients after work, a woodworker who sells cutting boards online. The side hustle is the testing ground where ideas are validated, skills are developed, and revenue is generated before the leap to full-time self-employment.

### Side hustle economics

The financial structure is different from a full business:
- **No overhead** (or nearly none): The side hustler works from home, uses personal equipment, and has no employees. [Operating expenses](./operating-expenses.md) are minimal.
- **No salary requirement**: The primary job covers living expenses. Revenue from the side hustle is supplemental — it doesn't need to pay rent or health insurance.
- **Low capital risk**: Most side hustles start with under $500 in materials and supplies. Failure costs time, not life savings.
- **Tax obligations still apply**: Side hustle income above $400/year triggers self-employment tax (15.3%). All income must be reported. See [Small Business Tax Strategy](../curricula/small-business-tax-strategy.md).

### The transition question

The central strategic question for any side hustle: does this become a full business, or does it stay a side income stream? The answer depends on whether the revenue potential justifies leaving a salary, benefits, and stability. See [Starting a Side Hustle](../curricula/starting-a-side-hustle.md).
