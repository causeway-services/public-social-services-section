A minimum viable product (MVP) is the simplest version of a product or service that can be offered to real customers for real money. It tests the core question — will people pay for this? — without investing in the full vision.

A home baker's MVP is not a full bakery. It's 24 cookies sold at a farmers' market. A freelance designer's MVP is not a design agency. It's three projects for paying clients. A meal prep service's MVP is not a commercial kitchen with delivery drivers. It's 10 meals delivered to 10 people in the neighborhood from a home kitchen.

### Why MVP matters for small businesses

The alternative to starting with an MVP is building the complete business before testing it: signing a lease, buying all the equipment, hiring staff, and opening the doors — only to discover that the market doesn't want the product, the location is wrong, or the price is off. An MVP lets you discover these things when the cost of being wrong is $200, not $200,000.

### What makes a good MVP

- **It's real**: Actual product sold to actual customers for actual money. A survey asking "would you buy this?" is not an MVP — people say yes to hypotheticals and no with their wallets.
- **It's minimal**: Only the core offering. A taco truck's MVP is three taco varieties and a drink. Not a full menu, not catering, not merchandise.
- **It generates feedback**: Every MVP sale is a data point. What did the customer say? Did they come back? What did they wish was different? What did they love?
- **It's improvable**: The MVP is version 1. Version 2 incorporates what was learned. Version 3 incorporates more. The product evolves toward what customers actually want — not what the owner imagined they'd want.

### MVP and the side hustle

For most [side hustles](./side-hustle.md), the MVP is the entire business for months or years. The home baker who sells at the Saturday market is running an MVP that generates income, validates demand, builds a customer base, and teaches the craft — all before any decision about scaling to a commercial operation is required.
