# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Acquisition premium.** What a buyer pays above a company's value on its
own, which the synergies must repay. [3]

**Asymmetric dependence.** One party having far fewer alternatives than the
other. [4]

**Backward induction.** Solving a sequential game from the last move back.
[5]

**Build, borrow, buy.** Organic growth; contracts and alliances; mergers and
acquisitions. [3]

**Chicken-and-egg problem.** Neither side of a platform joins without the
other. [6]

**Commitment.** A move that limits your own options to make a threat or
promise credible. [5]

**Complementor.** A player whose product makes yours more valuable. [1]

**Co-opetition.** Competing and cooperating with the same player: creating
value together, competing to divide it. [5]

**Course project; published takeaway.** The work a course asks you to
produce; what the course page says you'll be able to do. [7]

**Credibility; clarity.** Believing a partner will keep its word;
understanding what it expects. [4]

**Credible threat.** A threat the threatener would want to carry out when
the time comes. [5]

**Customer and supplier surplus.** Willingness to pay minus price; payment
minus opportunity cost. [1]

**Disintermediation.** A platform cutting out the businesses that sell
through it, for example by selling its own version of their products. [6]

**Disruptive innovation.** An entrant's product that starts worse on the
market's key measures but cheaper or simpler, with overlooked customers, and
improves until it takes the mainstream. [2]

**Dominant strategy.** A strategy best whatever others do. [5]

**Drift.** Each side's expectations changing without the other knowing. [4]

**Envelopment.** A larger platform bundling another platform's function into
its own. [6]

**Evidence; recommendation.** The facts behind each judgment in an analysis;
the choice it leads to. [7]

**Five forces.** Rivalry, new entrants, buyer power, supplier power,
substitutes. [1]

**Game tree; payoff table.** Pictures of sequential and simultaneous games.
[5]

**Gradual change; shock; uncertainty.** Slow shifts; sudden events; futures
that can go more than one way. [2]

**Hold-up problem.** A partner exploiting another's relation-specific
investment. [3, 4]

**Low-end and new-market footholds.** Where disruptive entrants start:
overserved customers, or people who weren't customers at all. [2]

**Market diagnosis.** Finding the target customers, their unmet needs, and
what raises their willingness to pay. [1]

**Modular, sequential and reciprocal synergies.** Pooled separate work; one
side's work feeding the other's; continuous close work together. [3]

**Multi-homing.** Using more than one platform at once. [6]

**Nash equilibrium.** Strategies from which no player gains by changing its
own alone. [5]

**Network effect.** A product becoming more valuable as more people use it;
same-side or cross-side. [6]

**Partner fit.** Strategic, resource, cultural and financial fit, and track
record. [3]

**PARTS.** Players, added values, rules, tactics, scope. [5]

**Platform.** A business that creates value by enabling interactions between
groups rather than by making a product itself. [6]

**Platform dependence.** Relying on a platform whose fees and rules you
don't control. [6]

**Platform governance.** The rules a platform sets and enforces for its
participants, which decide whether they trust it. [6]

**Price structure.** How a platform splits its total charges between its
sides. [6]

**Prisoner's dilemma.** A game in which each player's dominant strategy
leaves both worse off than cooperating. [5]

**Relational contract.** A shared understanding, much of it unwritten,
sustained by the value of the relationship's future. [4]

**Relation-specific assets.** Investments tailored to one partner. [4]

**Repeated game; tit for tat.** A game played many times; cooperating first,
then copying the other's last move. [5]

**Reputation.** What other players believe about how you'll behave, carried
from one round of a repeated game to the next. [5]

**Resistance to change.** Opposition with reasons: lost status or skill,
doubt, or being left out. [2]

**Resource-based view.** Advantage from resources that are valuable, rare,
inimitable and non-substitutable. [1]

**Scenario planning.** Testing strategies against a few plausible futures.
[2]

**Seeding.** Bringing one side of a platform yourself, such as signing
suppliers before buyers arrive. [6]

**Signpost.** An early sign of which scenario is unfolding. [2]

**Single-side value.** A reason for one side to join a platform before the
other side is there. [6]

**Subsidy side.** The side of a platform that pays little or nothing,
because the other side values its presence. [6]

**Supplier opportunity cost.** The least a supplier would accept, what it
could earn elsewhere. [1]

**Sustaining innovation.** Improvement for existing customers on the
measures they already value. [2]

**Synergy.** Extra value from combining two businesses: cost, revenue or
capability. [3]

**Tipping.** A market moving toward one dominant platform. [6]

**Transaction costs.** The costs of arranging and enforcing exchanges
between separate parties. [3]

**Two-sided market.** A platform serving two groups linked by cross-side
network effects. [6]

**Unfreeze, change, refreeze.** Lewin's three movements of change. [2]

**Value captured.** The share of value created that a firm keeps. [1]

**Value created.** Willingness to pay minus supplier opportunity cost. [1]

**Value net.** A business among its customers, suppliers, competitors and
complementors. [1]

**Willingness to pay.** The most a customer would pay. [1]

**Winner-take-all.** A market in which one platform ends up with most of the
users and most of the value. [6]
