# About this book {.unnumbered}

This book is a study companion for people taking Cornell University's
Business Strategy certificate on eCornell. It follows the certificate course
by course. For each course it states what you should be able to do when you
finish, teaches the ideas through one running business, and then asks you to
recall them before it shows you the answers.

It's for you if you're taking the certificate, deciding whether to, or have
finished it and want a compact review. It assumes you know what a business
does, not that you've studied economics.

## An independent companion

**This book is independent. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or
sponsored by Cornell University or eCornell.** "Cornell" and "eCornell" are
trademarks of Cornell University. The book names them only to say which
certificate it's a companion to.

It doesn't reproduce the courses' videos, readings, tools, discussions or
projects, and it can't stand in for them. You earn the certificate by
completing the courses. The book teaches the same subjects from the economics
and strategy research the field is built on, and asks its own questions.

online; its courses are authored by Justin P. Johnson, professor of economics
at the Cornell SC Johnson College of Business. Read from eCornell on 27 September 2026 [@ecornell-bs-2026]: about
three months at three to five hours a week; six two-week courses; 60
professional development hours (6 CEUs), also counted toward SHRM and HRCI
recertification; and a list price of $3,900, with payment plans. It includes

## The running example

> **FieldTable.** A four-year-old company in one region runs an online
> marketplace where about 120 small farms sell produce, meat and dairy to
> about 300 restaurants. FieldTable takes orders, arranges pickup and
> delivery through a local courier cooperative, and charges a fee on each
> order. Most restaurants also buy from Continental, a national food
> distributor with lower prices, wider range and its own trucks.

Each chapter looks at FieldTable's position from a different angle: how it
creates value, how its industry is changing, whom it should combine with,
which relationships matter, how its rival will respond, and how to run it as
a platform.

## How to use this book

Two findings from learning research shape the book. Recalling something from
memory strengthens it more than rereading it [@roediger2006]. Reviews spread
over time beat the same reviews crammed together, and the best gap grows with
how long you need to remember [@cepeda2006]. A large review of study
techniques rated these two, practice testing and spaced practice, the most
useful of the ten it examined, and rated rereading and highlighting among the
least [@dunlosky2013].

Each chapter opens with **What you will be able to do** and ends with **Check
yourself** questions and their **Answers**. Answer on paper before you look,
then come back to the same questions a few days later and again after a week;
the study plan fits that schedule to the certificate's two-week courses. The
courses ask you to apply each framework to your own organization; do the same
with the book's worked examples.

## What this book doesn't do

- It doesn't grant, or count toward, the Cornell certificate or its
  professional development hours.
- It doesn't replace the courses' instructors, discussions or tools.
- It isn't investment, legal or financial advice.
- It reflects the courses as described on 27 September 2026. If a course has
  changed, trust the course.
