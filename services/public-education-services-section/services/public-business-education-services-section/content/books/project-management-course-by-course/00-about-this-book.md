# About this book {.unnumbered}

This book is a study companion for people taking Google's Project Management
Professional Certificate on Coursera. It follows the certificate course by
course. For each course it states what you should be able to do when you
finish, teaches the ideas through one running example, and then asks you to
recall them before it shows you the answers.

It's for you if you're taking the certificate, thinking about taking it, or
already finished it and want a compact review before an interview or the
CAPM exam. It assumes no project management background.

## An independent companion

**This book is independent. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or
sponsored by Google, Coursera or the Project Management Institute (PMI).**
"Google" is a trademark of Google LLC and "Coursera" of Coursera, Inc. "PMI",
"CAPM" and "PMBOK" are registered marks of the Project Management Institute,
Inc. The book names them only to say which credential it's a companion to.

It doesn't reproduce the course's videos, readings, quizzes or graded
assignments, and it can't stand in for them. You earn the certificate by
completing the courses on Coursera. The book teaches the same subject from the
original sources the field is built on (the papers and standards cited in each
chapter) and asks its own questions.

## The certificate at a glance

Google publishes the certificate as seven courses. The hours are Google's own
estimates, read from the course pages on 27 September 2026 [@google-pm-2026]:

| # | Course | Hours | Chapters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations of Project Management | 13 | 1 |
| 2 | Project Initiation: Starting a Successful Project | 15 | 2 |
| 3 | Project Planning: Putting It All Together | 23 | 3 and 4 |
| 4 | Project Execution: Running the Project | 25 | 5 and 6 |
| 5 | Agile Project Management | 20 | 7 and 8 |
| 6 | Capstone: Applying Project Management in the Real World | 40 | 9 |
| 7 | Accelerate Your Job Search with AI | 6 | not covered |

Google states that people who finish the certificate have more than 100 hours
of project management education, which counts toward the 23 hours PMI requires
before its CAPM exam [@google-pm-2026]. Not every course hour counts (the
job-search course, for one, isn't project management education), but the
certificate clears PMI's requirement with room to spare. Coursera charges a monthly
subscription; check the current price on the certificate's page before you
enroll.

The seventh course is about job hunting, not project management, so this
book leaves it out.

## The running example

Every chapter works on the same invented project, so you can watch one
project move from idea to close:

> **The Eastside Weekend Pantry.** A regional food bank wants to open a
> weekend food pantry in the gym of Eastside Middle School, serving families
> who can't reach its weekday site. It has a budget of $12,000, a volunteer
> coordinator, a school principal who has said yes in principle, and eight
> weeks before the school year's second term starts.

The project is small on purpose. Large projects use the same tools with more
rows in each table.

## How to use this book

The book is built on two findings from learning research. First, recalling
something from memory strengthens it more than rereading it. Students who
practiced recalling a passage remembered more of it a week later than
students who spent the same time rereading it, even though the rereaders felt
more confident [@roediger2006]. Second, reviews spread out over time beat the
same reviews crammed together, and the best gap grows with how long you need
to remember [@cepeda2006]. A large review of study techniques rated these two,
practice testing and spaced practice, the most useful of the ten it examined,
and rated rereading and highlighting among the least [@dunlosky2013].

So each chapter ends with **Check yourself**, a short set of questions, and
then **Answers**. Use them this way:

1. Read the chapter once, alongside or after the matching course.
2. Close the book and answer the questions on paper, from memory.
3. Check your answers. Reread only the parts you got wrong.
4. Come back to the same questions a few days later, and again a week or two
   after that. The study plan at the end of the book lays out a schedule.

Each chapter opens with **What you will be able to do**. Those objectives are
this book's restatement of what the course sets out to teach. If you can do
each thing on the list without looking, you're ready for the course's graded
work on that topic.

## What this book doesn't do

- It doesn't grant, or count toward, the Google certificate or any PMI
  credential.
- It doesn't replace the hands-on templates and tools the courses have you
  use, such as spreadsheets for schedules and budgets.
- It doesn't cover every example or reading in the courses. It covers the
  ideas those examples teach.
- It reflects the courses as described on 27 September 2026. Coursera updates
  courses, so if a course has changed, trust the course.
