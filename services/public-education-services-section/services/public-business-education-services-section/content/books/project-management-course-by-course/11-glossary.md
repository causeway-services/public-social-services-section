# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**100 percent rule.** Each level of a work breakdown structure contains all
the work of the level above it, and the whole contains all of the project's
scope and nothing more. [3]

**Acceptance criteria.** The conditions a user story or deliverable must
meet to be accepted. [8]

**Actual cost (AC).** What the work done so far actually cost. [5]

**Adaptive approach (Agile).** Delivering in short cycles, planning in
detail only a little ahead, and adjusting from feedback. [1, 7]

**Budget at completion (BAC).** The total cost baseline. [5]

**Burndown chart.** A chart of work remaining in a sprint against days
left. [8]

**Change control.** The process of submitting, analyzing, deciding,
recording and communicating changes to the baseline. [5]

**Change management.** Helping the people a project affects adopt what it
delivers. [1]

**Charter.** The document that authorizes a project and states its purpose,
goals, scope, stakeholders, budget and risks. [2]

**Contingency reserve.** Budget set aside for identified risks, inside the
cost baseline. [4]

**Cost baseline.** The approved budget, without the management reserve, that
spending is measured against. [4]

**Cost performance index (CPI).** EV / AC. Below 1 means over budget. [5]

**Cost variance (CV).** EV − AC. Negative means over budget. [5]

**Critical path.** The longest path through a project's network of tasks; it
sets the project's duration. [3]

**Definition of Done.** The quality standard an increment must meet. [8]

**Deliverable.** A tangible output the project hands over. [2]

**Developers.** In Scrum, the people who create each increment; called the
"Development Team" before 2020. [7]

**Earned value (EV).** The budgeted cost of the work actually done so far.
[5]

**Epic.** A backlog item too large for one sprint, split into stories. [8]

**Escalation.** Asking someone with more authority to resolve a blocker
[5]; as a risk response, passing a risk outside the project's authority to
whoever owns it [4].

**Estimate at completion (EAC).** A forecast of total cost: BAC / CPI if
current performance continues, or AC + (BAC − EV) if the variance was a
one-off. [5]

**Float (slack).** How long a task can slip without delaying the project.
[3]

**Gantt chart.** A bar chart of tasks on a calendar. [3]

**Increment.** A usable step toward the Product Goal. [8]

**INVEST.** Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable: a
test for user stories. [8]

**Issue.** A problem that has happened. Compare risk. [4]

**Kanban.** A method of visualizing work, limiting work in progress and
managing flow. [8]

**Little's law.** Average items in a system = arrival rate × average time in
the system; so delivery time = WIP / throughput. [8]

**Management reserve.** Budget held by the sponsor for unforeseen work,
outside the cost baseline. [4]

**Milestone.** A point marking something important reached; it has no
duration. [3]

**Minimum viable product (MVP).** The version that teaches the most about
what customers want for the least effort. [8]

**MoSCoW.** Must, Should, Could, Won't have this time: a prioritization
scheme. [8]

**Net Promoter Score (NPS).** Percentage of promoters (9–10) minus
percentage of detractors (0–6) on a 0–10 recommendation question. [5]

**Operations.** Ongoing, repeated work that sustains an organization. [1]

**PERT expected time.** (o + 4m + p) / 6. [3]

**Plan-do-check-act (PDCA).** A cycle for testing and adopting
improvements. [5]

**Planned value (PV).** The budgeted cost of the work scheduled to be done
by now. [5]

**Portfolio.** All the programs, projects and operations an organization
invests in to serve its strategy. [1]

**Power-interest grid.** A stakeholder analysis tool that sets attention by
power over the project and interest in it. [2]

**Predictive approach (Waterfall).** Planning up front, then executing in
sequence. [1]

**Product Backlog.** The single ordered list of work that might improve the
product. [8]

**Product Owner.** The Scrum accountability for maximizing the product's
value. [7]

**Program.** Related projects managed together toward a shared goal. [1]

**Project.** A temporary effort to create a unique result. [1]

**Psychological safety.** A team's shared belief that it's safe to take
interpersonal risks there. [6]

**RACI chart.** A table assigning Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and
Informed roles for each task. [2]

**Retrospective.** A meeting to improve how the team works. [5, 8]

**Risk.** An uncertain event that would affect the project if it happened.
[4]

**Risk register.** The table of risks with scores, responses, owners and
triggers. [4]

**Schedule performance index (SPI).** EV / PV. Below 1 means behind. [5]

**Schedule variance (SV).** EV − PV. Negative means behind. [5]

**Scope; scope creep.** The project's boundary; its uncontrolled growth. [2]

**Scrum.** An Agile framework of three accountabilities, five events and
three artifacts. [7, 8]

**Scrum Master.** The Scrum accountability for the team's effectiveness. [7]

**SMART.** Specific, measurable, assignable (or achievable), realistic (or
relevant), time-related: a test for goals. [2]

**Sponsor.** The person who champions and funds the project and is
accountable for it. [2]

**Sprint.** Scrum's fixed-length cycle of one month or less. [8]

**Stakeholder.** Anyone who affects or is affected by the project. [2]

**Story points.** A relative measure of a story's size. [8]

**User story.** A short description of a need from a user's point of view.
[8]

**Velocity.** Story points a team completes per sprint. [8]

**Work breakdown structure (WBS).** A hierarchy that breaks a project's
scope into work packages. [3]

**Work in progress (WIP).** Items started and not finished. [8]
