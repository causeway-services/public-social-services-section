# A study plan {.unnumbered}

This plan spaces your practice across the weeks you spend on the
certificate. It follows the finding that reviews spread over time outlast the
same reviews crammed together, and that the best gap between reviews grows
with how long you need to remember [@cepeda2006]. It uses the Check yourself
questions as practice tests, the most useful single study technique in the
review of ten techniques this book's introduction cites [@dunlosky2013].

## The rule

After you finish each chapter, answer its Check yourself questions from
memory three times:

1. **The next day.**
2. **About three days later.**
3. **About a week after that.**

Each time, write your answers before you look. Mark the ones you missed, and
reread only the sections that answer them. If you get every question right
twice in a row, you can drop that chapter from the schedule until the final
review.

## A schedule at ten hours a week

Coursera sets its own pace estimates; this schedule assumes about ten hours a
week and gives the course hours from its pages. Adjust the weeks to your own
pace, and keep the review gaps.

| Weeks | Course | Read | Review |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 1 Foundations (13 h) | Chapter 1 | Chapter 1 on days 1, 4, 11 |
| 3–4 | 2 Initiation (15 h) | Chapter 2 | Chapter 2 on days 1, 4, 11; Chapter 1 once |
| 5–7 | 3 Planning (23 h) | Chapters 3 and 4 | Each on days 1, 4, 11; Chapter 2 once |
| 8–10 | 4 Execution (25 h) | Chapters 5 and 6 | Each on days 1, 4, 11; Chapters 3 and 4 once |
| 11–12 | 5 Agile (20 h) | Chapters 7 and 8 | Each on days 1, 4, 11; Chapters 5 and 6 once |
| 13–16 | 6 Capstone (40 h) | Chapter 9 before each module | Chapter 9's checklist on each artifact before you submit it |

"Once" means one pass through that chapter's questions during those weeks:
the older chapters come back at longer gaps.

## The final review

In the week after the capstone, answer every chapter's questions in one
sitting, mixed rather than in order. Mixing topics makes you decide which
idea each question needs, which is what interviews and exams ask of you.
Reread only what you missed.

## If you're preparing for an interview

Two weeks before: do the final review. Then pick three artifacts from your
capstone portfolio and practice telling each as a story in under two minutes:
the situation, what you did, and the result.

## If you're going on to the CAPM

The certificate's hours count toward the CAPM's education requirement, but
the exam covers more: process groups, earned value in depth, business
analysis, and a wider range of Agile frameworks. The second book in this
series, *The CAPM, Domain by Domain*, follows the exam's own outline and
includes a practice exam.
