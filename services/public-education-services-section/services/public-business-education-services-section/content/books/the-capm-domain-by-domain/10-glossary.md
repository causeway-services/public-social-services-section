# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Acceptance criteria.** Conditions a specific deliverable or backlog item
must meet to be accepted. [4, 6]

**Activity.** A scheduled piece of work that produces part of a work
package. [3]

**Adaptive life cycle.** Refines requirements and delivers usable increments
each cycle; Agile. [1, 4]

**Agile Release Train.** In SAFe, a team of Agile teams that plans and
delivers together. [4]

**Assumption.** Something believed true for planning, without proof. [1]

**Baseline.** An approved version of scope, schedule or cost, changed only
through change control. [2]

**Benefits management plan.** Says what benefits a project should bring, who
owns them, when and how they're measured. [2]

**Burnup chart.** Plots work completed against total scope over time. [4]

**Business analysis.** Understanding a need and defining a solution that
meets it. [5]

**Business case.** The justification for a project. [2]

**Charter.** The document that authorizes a project and names its project
manager. [2, 3]

**Constraint.** A limit the project must work within. [1]

**Cost of quality.** Costs of conformance (prevention, appraisal) and
nonconformance (internal and external failure). [3]

**Crashing.** Adding resources to critical-path activities to shorten the
schedule, at a cost. [3]

**Critical path.** The longest path through the schedule network; sets the
shortest project duration. [3]

**Cumulative flow diagram.** Shows the number of items in each state over
time. [4]

**Definition of done.** The quality standard every increment must meet. [4]

**Deliverable.** A product, result or capability a project must produce.
[3]

**Earned value management.** Comparing planned value, earned value and actual
cost. [3]

**Elicitation.** Drawing requirements out of stakeholders and sources. [5]

**Emotional intelligence.** Recognizing and managing your own emotions and
others'. [2]

**Enterprise environmental factors (EEFs).** Conditions outside the team's
control that affect the project. [4]

**Epic.** A large body of work split into user stories. [4]

**Fast tracking.** Running planned-sequential activities in parallel, at a
risk. [3]

**Float (total).** How long an activity can slip without delaying the
project: LS − ES. [3]

**Hybrid.** A project mixing predictive and adaptive approaches. [1]

**Increment.** A usable piece of the product delivered in an iteration. [4]

**Integrated change control.** Reviewing each change for its effect on all
baselines and plans at once. [3]

**Issue.** A current problem needing action. [1]

**Kanban.** A flow method: visualize work, limit WIP, pull work. [4]

**Knowledge areas.** Ten groupings of project management processes, from
integration to stakeholders. [1]

**Milestone.** A significant point with zero duration. [2]

**Minimum viable product (MVP).** The smallest version of a product that
delivers value and teaches the team what users want. [6]

**Operations.** Ongoing work that sustains the organization. [1]

**Organizational process assets (OPAs).** The organization's own plans,
processes, templates and knowledge. [4]

**Phase.** A stretch of the life cycle ending in a major deliverable or
decision. [1, 3]

**Portfolio.** Projects, programs and operations managed together for
strategic objectives. [1]

**Process groups.** Initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and
controlling, closing. [1]

**Process manager; process owner.** Runs a process day to day; is accountable
for it end to end. [5]

**Product backlog.** The ordered list of work for a product. [4, 5]

**Product management plan.** Covers a product across its whole life. [2]

**Product manager; product owner.** Owns a product's strategy and roadmap;
maximizes value by ordering an Agile team's backlog. [5]

**Product roadmap.** A high-level view of a product's direction and releases.
[6]

**Program.** Related projects managed together for benefits. [1]

**Project.** A temporary endeavor to create a unique product, service or
result. [1]

**Requirements traceability matrix (RTM).** Links each requirement to its
source and to design, build and test. [5, 6]

**Risk.** An uncertain future event that would affect objectives. [1]

**Scope baseline.** The scope statement, WBS and WBS dictionary. [2, 3]

**Servant leadership.** Leading by serving the team and removing obstacles.
[4]

**Stakeholder register.** Records stakeholders' identification, assessment
and classification. [2]

**Subsidiary plan.** A plan for managing one area of the project, such as
scope, schedule, cost or risk. [2, 3]

**Transition requirements.** Temporary needs for moving to the future state.
[5]

**Tuckman's stages.** Forming, storming, norming, performing and
adjourning: how teams tend to develop. [2]

**Use case.** A step-by-step description of an actor's interaction with a
system, with alternate paths. [5]

**User story.** A short statement of a need from a user's point of view. [4,
5]

**Validation; verification.** Checking the product meets the need; checking
it meets its specification. [6]

**Velocity.** Work completed per iteration. [4]

**WBS; work package; WBS dictionary.** The deliverable hierarchy; its lowest
level; its detailed descriptions. [3]
