# About this book {.unnumbered}

This book is a study companion for PMI's Certified Associate in Project
Management (CAPM)® exam. It follows the exam's own published outline, domain
by domain and task by task. For each part it states what you need to be able
to do, teaches it through one running example, and then asks you to recall it
before it shows you the answers. It ends with a 40-question practice exam in
the same proportions as the real one.

It's for you if you're preparing for the CAPM, whether you're new to project
management or have finished a course such as Google's Project Management
certificate and want to close the gaps between that course and the exam.

## An independent companion

**This book is independent. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or
sponsored by the Project Management Institute (PMI).** "PMI", "CAPM", "PMP"
and "PMBOK" are registered marks of the Project Management Institute, Inc.
The book names them only to say which exam it's a companion to.

It doesn't contain PMI exam questions, and no question in it has appeared on
an exam. Every question was written for this book. It restates the exam's
published outline in its own words, with attribution, and teaches the subject
from the standards and original sources listed in each chapter.

## Which version of the exam this book covers

PMI publishes what the CAPM tests in its **Examination Content Outline**.
This book follows the outline's 2023 update [@pmi-capm-eco-2023], which is
the version in force on 27 September 2026, when the book was written.

PMI released the eighth edition of its *PMBOK Guide* in November 2025. As of
this writing, PMI hasn't published a new CAPM outline to match it; the 2023
outline and its reference list, which cites the seventh edition, still govern
the exam. If PMI publishes a new outline, compare its domains and weights
with the table below before relying on this book.

## The exam at a glance

The outline divides the exam into four domains [@pmi-capm-eco-2023]:

| Domain | Share of questions | Chapters here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Project Management Fundamentals and Core Concepts | 36% | 1 and 2 |
| 2. Predictive, Plan-Based Methodologies | 17% | 3 |
| 3. Agile Frameworks/Methodologies | 20% | 4 |
| 4. Business Analysis Frameworks | 27% | 5 and 6 |

Each domain is broken into **tasks**, the responsibilities the exam tests,
with example **enablers** under each. Every exam covers every task. The
outline also says that predictive, adaptive and business analysis approaches
appear throughout all four domains, not only in their own. So a question
filed under fundamentals may well describe an Agile team.

The format, from the same outline:

- 150 questions, of which 135 are scored and 15 are unscored pretest
  questions you can't tell apart from the rest;
- 3 hours, with an optional 10-minute break after question 75; once you take
  the break, you can't return to the first 75;
- multiple choice, drag-and-drop matching, "hot spot" questions where you
  click part of an image, and short animations or comic strips that set up a
  scenario (online exams use only comic strips);
- up to three attempts within a one-year eligibility period; after three
  attempts without passing, you must wait a year from your last exam before
  reapplying.

To be eligible you need a secondary diploma or equivalent and 23 hours of
project management education completed before the exam [@pmi-capm-eco-2023]. PMI sets the fees;
training providers reported US$225 for PMI members and US$300 for others in
September 2026, with renewal every three years through 15 professional
development units. Check PMI's site for the current figures.

## The references the exam draws on

The outline says every question is sourced in at least two of seven
references, and names them: the *PMBOK Guide*, seventh edition
[@pmbok7]; *Process Groups: A Practice Guide* [@pmi-process-groups2022];
*The PMI Guide to Business Analysis* [@pmi-ba-guide2017]; *Business Analysis
for Practitioners: A Practice Guide*, second edition [@pmi-ba-practitioners2024];
the *Agile Practice Guide* [@agile-practice-guide2017]; *The Project Management
Answer Book*, second edition [@furman2015]; and *Effective Project Management*,
eighth edition [@wysocki2019].

You don't need to read all of them. This book teaches what they share and
names the source when one of them uses a term in a particular way.

## The running example

> **The Riverside Clinic booking project.** A community health clinic takes
> appointments only by phone, and patients wait on hold. The clinic's
> director wants patients to book online and get text reminders. The project
> has a budget of $80,000, a front-desk team that must keep working
> throughout, a software vendor to choose, and patients who range from
> teenagers to people in their nineties.

The project mixes predictive work (choosing a vendor, installing hardware,
training staff) with adaptive work (designing the booking screens with
patients), and it needs business analysis throughout. That mix is what the
exam tests.

## How to use this book

Two findings from learning research shape the book. Recalling something from
memory strengthens it more than rereading it [@roediger2006]. Reviews spread
over time beat the same reviews crammed together, and the best gap grows with
how long you need to remember [@cepeda2006]. A large review of study
techniques rated practice testing and spaced practice the most useful of the
ten it examined, and rated rereading and highlighting among the least
[@dunlosky2013].

So each chapter opens with **What you will be able to do**, the outline's
tasks restated as things you can check yourself on, and ends with **Check
yourself** questions in the exam's scenario style, followed by **Answers**
that explain why each wrong option is wrong. Answer on paper before you look.
Come back to the same questions after a few days and again a week or two
later; the study plan lays out a schedule by weeks to your exam date. Take
the practice exam in Chapter 7 once, timed, about two weeks before your exam.

## What this book doesn't do

- It doesn't make you eligible for the exam; the 23 education hours must
  come from a course, and PMI's rules say which kinds count.
- It doesn't contain real exam questions, and it can't promise a pass.
- It doesn't replace PMI's own references for depth.
- It reflects the 2023 outline, the version in force when it was written.
