# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Accounting equation.** Assets = liabilities + equity. [1]

**Accounting; finance.** Recording and reporting what has happened to a
business's money; deciding what to do next with it. [1]

**Accrual accounting.** Recording revenue when earned and expenses when
incurred, whether or not cash has moved. [1]

**Balance sheet.** Assets, liabilities and equity at one moment. [1]

**Centralization; formalization.** How far decisions are made at the top;
how far work follows written rules. [4]

**Chain of command; departmentalization.** Who reports to whom; how jobs are
grouped (by function, product, customer, place or matrix). [4]

**Common-size and trend analysis.** Each line as a share of revenue;
comparing the same measures across years. [1]

**Coordination mechanisms.** Mutual adjustment, direct supervision and
standardization of processes, outputs or skills. [4]

**Cost leadership; differentiation; focus.** Porter's generic strategies.
[3]

**Culture.** Artifacts, espoused values and basic underlying assumptions.
[4]

**Current ratio; quick ratio.** Current assets over current liabilities,
with and without inventory. [1]

**Customer acquisition cost (CAC); customer lifetime value (CLV).** The
average cost of winning a customer; the profit a customer brings over the
relationship. [2]

**Debt-to-equity.** Total liabilities over equity. [1]

**Disruptive innovation.** An innovation that starts worse but cheaper or
simpler with overlooked customers and improves until it takes the
mainstream. [6]

**Division of labor.** Splitting work into specialized jobs. [4]

**Double-entry bookkeeping.** Recording every transaction as an equal debit
and credit. [1]

**Ethics frameworks.** Utilitarian (greatest net benefit), rights (respect
for rights), justice (fair distribution), caring (honoring relationships)
and virtue (what a good person would do). [6]

**Expectancy theory.** Effort depends on expectancy, instrumentality and
valence. [5]

**Generic strategies.** See cost leadership; differentiation; focus. [3]

**Goal setting.** Specific, difficult, accepted goals with feedback raise
performance. [5]

**Gross, operating and net margin.** Gross profit, operating income and net
income over revenue. [1]

**Human, social and structural capital.** Skills; relationships and trust;
knowledge built into processes. [5]

**Ideation; screening.** Generating many ideas without judging; then
evaluating them by fit, demand, cost and risk. [6]

**Income statement.** Revenue, costs and profit over a period. [1]

**Interests and positions.** Why someone wants something; what they say they
want. [5]

**Job analysis; job description; person specification.** Studying a job;
describing it; describing the person it needs. [4]

**Lead with the point.** Putting the request or conclusion first in business
writing. [6]

**Marketing brief; organizational brief.** Plans for marketing, and for
organizing to carry out a strategy. [2, 4, 7]

**Marketing mix (4 Ps).** Product, price, place, promotion. [2]

**Matrix structure.** Grouping jobs two ways at once, such as by function
and by product. [4]

**Motivators and hygiene factors.** Herzberg's sources of satisfaction and
of dissatisfaction. [5]

**Networking strategy.** Deciding a goal, mapping ties, finding where people
gather, offering help first and keeping in touch. [6]

**Plain language; tone; channel.** Short sentences and common words; a
register that fits the relationship; the medium chosen for a message. [6]

**Positioning statement.** How an offer should be seen by its target,
relative to alternatives. [2]

**Power, bases of.** Legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent,
informational. [4]

**Psychic distance.** How different a foreign market feels. [6]

**Psychological safety.** A team's shared belief that it's safe to speak up.
[5]

**Real team; mutual accountability.** A small group with complementary
skills committed to a common purpose, goals and approach; members holding
each other, not only the leader, to account. [5]

**Return on assets (ROA); return on equity (ROE).** Net income over total
assets; over equity. [1]

**Segmentation, targeting and positioning.** Dividing a market, choosing
segments, and placing the offer in their minds. [2]

**Span of control.** The number of people reporting to one manager. [4]

**Stages of internationalization.** No regular exports; exporting through
agents; a sales subsidiary; production abroad. [6]

**Stakeholder.** A group that affects or is affected by the firm. [3]

**Stuck in the middle.** Pursuing both low cost and differentiation without
achieving either. [3]

**Sustaining innovation.** An improvement for a business's existing
customers on the measures they already value. [6]

**SWOT.** Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. [3]

**Task and relationship conflict.** Disagreement about the work; personal
friction. [5]

**Value chain.** The primary and support activities that create value. [3]

**Weak ties.** Acquaintances who carry new information. [6]
