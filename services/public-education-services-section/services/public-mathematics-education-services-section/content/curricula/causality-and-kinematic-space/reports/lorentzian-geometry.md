# Lorentzian geometry: cones, intervals, and diamonds

## Teaching question

How does a geometry represent possible influence when its metric distinguishes
timelike, null, and spacelike directions instead of measuring every separation
as an ordinary positive distance?

## Source-grounded synthesis

A Lorentzian metric assigns a signed quadratic form to tangent vectors. With a
fixed sign convention, vectors are timelike, null, or spacelike according to
that sign. The null directions form a light cone. Choosing a time orientation
then distinguishes future-directed from past-directed causal curves. These
local cones constrain which events can influence which others, but a cone drawn
on a page is only a coordinate picture: the causal classification and its
conformal structure are the invariant content relevant here.

Minguzzi and Sánchez organize the standard causal ladder—chronology, causality,
strong causality, stable causality, and global hyperbolicity—and emphasize that
distinct theorems require distinct hypotheses. A causal diamond between events
`p` and `q` is the intersection of the causal future of `p` with the causal past
of `q`. Its compactness appears in common formulations of global hyperbolicity,
but neither an arbitrary diamond-shaped drawing nor an arbitrary partial order
is therefore a globally hyperbolic spacetime.

Ryu and Takayanagi relate boundary-region entanglement entropy to the area of a
bulk minimal surface in static holographic settings. Hubeny, Rangamani, and
Takayanagi propose the covariant replacement by an extremal surface in
time-dependent asymptotically AdS spacetimes. These papers matter to this
course because later kinematic constructions inherit their Lorentzian and
holographic assumptions. They do not establish that every causal system has an
entropy-area dual or a kinematic space.

## Worked control

In two-dimensional Minkowski coordinates with line element
`ds² = -dt² + dx²`, the displacement from `(0,0)` to `(2,1)` is timelike,
to `(1,1)` is null, and to `(1,2)` is spacelike. Rescaling coordinates or
drawing axes at another angle may change the picture, but not a correctly
transformed interval classification. The causal diamond of `(0,0)` and `(2,0)`
is bounded by null lines; its boundary is not a Euclidean square with an
intrinsic side length read from the page.

## Scope limits and negative control

- This packet teaches smooth Lorentzian geometry; singular, discrete, and
  quantum geometries need additional definitions.
- Global causal properties do not follow from one tangent-space light cone.
- Holographic entropy prescriptions require the cited gravitational and
  boundary-theory settings.
- Invalid inference: “two events lie inside a drawn diamond, so their proper
  time is determined by the diamond’s pixel height.” Coordinates and screen
  dimensions are not invariant spacetime observables.

## Cinematic direction

Animate a Euclidean compass failing as one axis becomes time. Grow light cones
at successive events, trace timelike and null curves, and close two cones into
a causal diamond. Distort the coordinate grid while preserving causal type.
End by showing a boundary interval and its associated bulk extremal curve as a
carefully labeled special construction, not a universal analogy.
