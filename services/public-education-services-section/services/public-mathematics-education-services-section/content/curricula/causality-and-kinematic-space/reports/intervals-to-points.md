# From intervals to points

## Teaching question

How can an extended boundary interval or a bulk geodesic become a single point
of another space without erasing the information that made it extended?

## Source-grounded construction

In the AdS3/CFT2 setting used by Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully, an
oriented boundary-anchored bulk geodesic can be labeled by its two boundary
endpoints. The ordered endpoint pair is then a point of a two-dimensional
kinematic space. Equivalently, on a spatial boundary slice it labels a boundary
interval. The construction is a change in what counts as a point: kinematic
space does not shrink the original interval inside the boundary geometry; it
uses the interval’s identity as an elementary object in a derived geometry.

The endpoint coordinates are not arbitrary independent coordinates. Exchanging
orientation, approaching coincident endpoints, crossing a periodic coordinate
seam, or changing the bulk state affects the domain and interpretation. In
vacuum AdS3, the resulting kinematic metric has a de Sitter form. Asplund,
Callebaut, and Zukowski compare emergent de Sitter descriptions across vacuum,
thermal, and nontrivial three-dimensional gravitational states, while retaining
state- and phase-dependent qualifications.

Czech and collaborators also interpret OPE blocks as fields on kinematic
space. At leading semiclassical order, those objects can be related to
geodesic or minimal-surface integrals. This enriches the interval-as-point
construction but does not turn every pair of points in every field theory into
a smooth Lorentzian kinematic manifold.

## Worked parameterization

On a circular boundary, describe an ordered interval by endpoints `(u,v)` with
`u < v` on a chosen lift, or by midpoint and half-opening angle
`θ = (u+v)/2`, `α = (v-u)/2`. A single pair `(θ,α)` is one kinematic point.
Varying `θ` moves the interval around the boundary; varying `α` changes its
size. The limit `α → 0` approaches a degenerate interval and often a boundary
of the kinematic domain rather than an ordinary interior point.

## Scope limits and negative control

- The interval/geodesic identification here uses AdS3/CFT2 and its relevant
  geodesic and entropy assumptions.
- Multiple bulk geodesics or phase transitions can make a naive endpoint label
  insufficient unless a branch is specified.
- Orientation and quotient identifications must be handled explicitly.
- Invalid inference: “each interval is one point, therefore the interval has
  become dimensionless in the original boundary theory.” It is a point only in
  the derived kinematic space.

## Cinematic direction

Sweep pairs of luminous endpoints around a boundary circle. For every pair,
draw the associated bulk geodesic, then lift that whole interval-geodesic object
into one point on a second surface. Animate midpoint motion along one direction
and interval size along another. Show coincident endpoints approaching the
domain boundary and a phase-transition branch splitting, with hypotheses
visible rather than hidden.
