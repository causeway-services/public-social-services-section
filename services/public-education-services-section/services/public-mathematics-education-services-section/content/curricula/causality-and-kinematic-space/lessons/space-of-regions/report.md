# Geometry on a space of regions

## Teaching question

Once intervals or geodesics are points, what can adjacency, causal order,
measure, distance, and curvature mean on their kinematic space?

## Source-grounded synthesis

Kinematic space inherits relations from how its represented intervals are
nested, intersect, share endpoints, or vary. On a fixed boundary time slice,
inclusion supplies a partial order: one interval can contain another, while
overlapping intervals may be incomparable. In the standard two-dimensional
holographic examples, this inclusion structure aligns with a Lorentzian causal
organization on kinematic space. The analogy has mathematical content because
the endpoint coordinates and entropy derivatives define the relevant form; it
is not licensed merely by drawing intervals above one another.

Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully use a Crofton form on the space of bulk
geodesics. Integrating that form over the set of geodesics intersecting a bulk
curve computes the curve’s length in the relevant AdS3 setting. A point curve
in the bulk corresponds not to one kinematic point but to a family of geodesics
passing through it, represented as a curve in kinematic space. Thus incidence
geometry exchanges the roles of points and families of extended objects.

Asplund, Callebaut, and Zukowski analyze how related de Sitter geometries arise
from entanglement, causal diamonds, and MERA in selected CFT states. Czech and
collaborators argue that MERA’s graph should be understood as a discretization
of kinematic space—the space of bulk geodesics—rather than simply the bulk
spatial geometry itself. This distinction prevents a common category error:
the adjacency of tensors or intervals is not automatically adjacency of bulk
points.

## Worked incidence example

Take a convex bulk curve. Every boundary-anchored geodesic that intersects it
defines a point in a region of kinematic space. A Crofton integral weighs that
region and recovers the curve length under the cited normalization and
geometry. If the curve deforms, the intersecting-geodesic region changes. The
measure is therefore attached to a set of represented geodesics, not to the
area of a hand-drawn patch on an arbitrary coordinate chart.

## Scope limits and negative control

- The Crofton reconstruction uses the cited holographic and geometric setting.
- Inclusion order, Lorentzian causal order, a metric, and a measure are related
  but not interchangeable structures.
- Tensor-network discretizations are model-dependent and do not establish an
  exact identity between every network and a continuum geometry.
- Invalid inference: “neighboring kinematic points represent neighboring bulk
  points.” They represent nearby geodesics or intervals; bulk-point incidence
  is generally encoded by a family of them.

## Cinematic direction

Fill a boundary circle with intervals and lift them into a curved kinematic
surface. Show nested intervals linked causally, intersecting intervals as a
different relation, and a bulk point becoming a whole kinematic curve. Sweep a
bulk curve through space while the region of intersecting geodesics lights up;
integrate its measure into length. Pixelate the surface into a MERA-like network
and label it as a discretization claim with limits.
