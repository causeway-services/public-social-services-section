# Modular Flow as a Vector Field on Kinematic Space

## 1. The question in context

Modular flow is a one-parameter evolution associated with a quantum state and a chosen spatial region. Kinematic space is a different kind of arena: its “points” are not ordinary spacetime events but extended objects such as intervals, causal diamonds, or—in holographic theories—bulk geodesics. The central question is therefore a change-of-level question:

> Given the modular flow of one causal diamond, how does that flow act on the space of all diamonds, and what can the resulting vector field reveal about entanglement?

The cleanest answer is known for the vacuum of a two-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT). An interval \(A=(a,b)\) determines a causal diamond and a modular Hamiltonian. Vacuum modular flow is generated by a conformal transformation that fixes \(a\) and \(b\). Acting with that transformation on the endpoints \((u,v)\) of every other interval produces a vector field on interval space:

\[
V_A
=
\zeta_A(u)\,\partial_u+\zeta_A(v)\,\partial_v,
\qquad
\zeta_A(x)=2\pi\,\frac{(x-a)(b-x)}{b-a}.
\]

This is the basic encoding. The integral curves of \(V_A\) are families of intervals carried into one another by the modular flow of \(A\). The reference interval \((a,b)\) is a fixed point; loci on which one endpoint equals \(a\) or \(b\) behave like kinematic horizons; and the signs of the two components distinguish intervals lying inside, outside, or across the reference diamond.

This construction is exact in the vacuum CFT setting because the modular Hamiltonian is local and conformally generated. It is not a universal formula for arbitrary quantum states or regions. The limits of that statement are as important as the construction itself.

## 2. Prerequisites: causal order without metric distance

A causal relation is fundamentally an ordering relation, not a distance. In a spacetime, write

\[
p\preceq q
\]

when \(q\) lies in or on the causal future of \(p\). Under standard causal assumptions this relation is reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric, so it is a partial order. Many pairs of events are incomparable: neither can causally influence the other.

The order tells us which events may influence which. By itself it does not assign a proper time, a spatial length, or a conformal scale. Indeed, conformally related Lorentzian metrics have the same null cones and therefore the same causal order, while their metric lengths generally differ. Inferring a numerical distance solely from causal comparability is consequently invalid unless additional information—such as a volume measure—is supplied.

For two timelike-separated events \(p\prec q\), their causal diamond is

\[
D(p,q)=J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q).
\]

For an interval \(A=(a,b)\) on a Cauchy slice of a \(1+1\)-dimensional spacetime, the domain of dependence \(D(A)\) is the diamond consisting of events whose physics is determined by data on \(A\). Its null boundaries are generated by light rays from the endpoints.

This distinction will recur at the kinematic level. Interval inclusion gives a partial order on regions, but that order does not by itself fix the normalization of a kinematic metric.

## 3. Modular Hamiltonians and modular flow

For a factorized finite-dimensional system, a state \(\rho\) and subsystem \(A\) give a reduced density matrix

\[
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_{A^c}\rho.
\]

Its modular Hamiltonian is conventionally

\[
K_A=-\log\rho_A,
\]

up to an additive constant if normalization is being suppressed. Modular flow acts on an operator \(O\) by

\[
O(s)=e^{isK_A}Oe^{-isK_A}.
\]

In relativistic quantum field theory this notation requires care: local algebras generally do not factorize into ordinary tensor factors, and their reduced density matrices are not always literal trace-class matrices. Tomita–Takesaki modular theory supplies the algebraic definition. The intuitive density-matrix notation remains useful when its limitations are remembered.

For a generic state and region, \(K_A\) is highly nonlocal. The vacuum of a CFT and a ball-shaped region are exceptional. Casini, Huerta, and Myers showed that the causal development of a ball can be conformally mapped to a hyperbolic cylinder, where the vacuum restricted to the ball becomes a thermal state; this explains why the corresponding modular evolution is geometric and why its generator can be written locally in terms of the stress tensor ([Casini, Huerta, and Myers, 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0440)).

For a vacuum CFT on Minkowski space and a ball \(B\) of radius \(R\), centered at \(\mathbf{x}_0\) on \(t=0\), the region’s modular Hamiltonian has the local form

\[
K_B
=
2\pi
\int_{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|<R}
d^{d-1}x\,
\frac{R^2-|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0|^2}{2R}
\,T_{00}(0,\mathbf{x})
+\text{constant}.
\]

In \(1+1\) dimensions, a ball is an interval \(A=(a,b)\), with \(R=(b-a)/2\). The formula reduces to

\[
K_A
=
2\pi\int_a^b dx\,
\frac{(x-a)(b-x)}{b-a}\,T_{00}(0,x)
+\text{constant}.
\]

A closely related stress-tensor expression for the vacuum modular generator of a single interval is displayed explicitly in the analysis of modular eigenmodes by Das, Ezhuthachan, Porey, and Roy ([Das et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10211)).

The weight

\[
\frac{(x-a)(b-x)}{b-a}
\]

vanishes at the interval endpoints and is positive inside. Its vanishing is not decorative: the endpoints, and hence the null boundary of the causal diamond, are preserved by the modular flow.

## 4. The spacetime flow inside a diamond

Let the reference interval be \(A=(a,b)\). On the \(t=0\) line, the infinitesimal conformal transformation generated by vacuum modular flow is

\[
\frac{dx}{ds}
=
\zeta_A(x),
\qquad
\zeta_A(x)
=
2\pi\frac{(x-a)(b-x)}{b-a}.
\]

The normalization of \(s\) is conventional; moving a factor of \(2\pi\) between the parameter and generator changes no orbit.

The fixed points satisfy \(\zeta_A(x)=0\), so they are precisely \(x=a\) and \(x=b\). Separating variables gives

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\log\frac{x-a}{b-x}=2\pi.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{x(s)-a}{b-x(s)}
=
e^{2\pi s}
\frac{x(0)-a}{b-x(0)}.
\]

As \(s\to-\infty\), a point inside the interval approaches \(a\); as \(s\to+\infty\), it approaches \(b\). It never crosses either endpoint at finite modular parameter.

In spacetime, the associated conformal Killing vector can be written

\[
\xi_A
=
\frac{2\pi}{b-a}
\left[
\bigl((x-a)(b-x)-t^2\bigr)\partial_t
-
t(2x-a-b)\partial_x
\right],
\]

up to an overall sign tied to the direction chosen for modular time. It is timelike in the diamond interior, null on its boundary, and vanishes at the diamond’s tips. Thus modular time is adapted to the diamond rather than to inertial time.

This geometric action is special. For a deformed region, an excited state, a massive theory, or a generic interacting QFT without the relevant conformal symmetry, modular flow normally ceases to be a local spacetime diffeomorphism.

## 5. Constructing kinematic space

On a fixed time slice, define kinematic space

\[
\mathcal K=\{(u,v)\mid u<v\},
\]

where the point \((u,v)\) represents the interval \((u,v)\). It may equivalently label that interval’s causal diamond. In holographic \(\mathrm{AdS}_3/\mathrm{CFT}_2\), an oriented boundary interval also labels the oriented bulk geodesic anchored at \(u\) and \(v\). Czech and collaborators formulate these identifications explicitly: kinematic space can be regarded as a space of boundary causal diamonds or oriented \(\mathrm{AdS}_3\) geodesics ([Czech et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03110)).

Introduce center and half-width coordinates,

\[
\theta=\frac{u+v}{2},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{v-u}{2}>0.
\]

Then increasing \(\alpha\) moves toward larger intervals, while changing \(\theta\) translates their centers.

There is a natural containment order:

\[
(u_1,v_1)\preceq_{\mathcal K}(u_2,v_2)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(u_1,v_1)\subseteq(u_2,v_2).
\]

In endpoint coordinates, this means

\[
u_2\le u_1,
\qquad
v_1\le v_2.
\]

On the de Sitter representation of vacuum kinematic space, nested intervals are timelike related, intervals sharing one endpoint are null related, and intervals with crossing endpoint order are spacelike related. The static-slice kinematic space of vacuum \(\mathrm{AdS}_3\) is two-dimensional de Sitter space, as established in the integral-geometric construction of Czech, Lamprou, McCandlish, and Sully ([Czech et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05515)).

Again, the order is not the metric. Inclusion determines the light-cone structure of \(\mathcal K\), but an overall conformal factor and length scale require more data.

## 6. Lifting modular flow to a kinematic vector field

Let \(f_s\) denote the modular conformal transformation generated by \(\zeta_A\). It sends any test interval

\[
I=(u,v)
\]

to

\[
I(s)=\bigl(f_s(u),f_s(v)\bigr).
\]

Differentiating at \(s=0\) gives the vector field

\[
\boxed{
V_A
=
\zeta_A(u)\partial_u+\zeta_A(v)\partial_v
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\zeta_A(x)
=
2\pi\frac{(x-a)(b-x)}{b-a}.
}
\]

This is the most direct representation of the modular flow of \(A\) on kinematic space. It is simply the diagonal action of the same conformal transformation on both endpoints.

For the symmetric reference interval \(A=(-R,R)\),

\[
\zeta_A(x)=\pi\,\frac{R^2-x^2}{R},
\]

so

\[
V_A
=
\frac{\pi}{R}
\left[
(R^2-u^2)\partial_u
+
(R^2-v^2)\partial_v
\right].
\]

In \((\theta,\alpha)\) coordinates this becomes

\[
V_A
=
\frac{\pi}{R}
\left[
(R^2-\theta^2-\alpha^2)\partial_\theta
-
2\theta\alpha\,\partial_\alpha
\right].
\]

Several features are now visible.

First, the reference interval is a fixed point:

\[
V_A\big|_{(u,v)=(-R,R)}=0.
\]

Second, linearization around the general fixed point \((a,b)\) gives

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\delta u=2\pi\,\delta u,
\qquad
\frac{d}{ds}\delta v=-2\pi\,\delta v.
\]

The fixed point is therefore saddle-like: one endpoint perturbation grows while the other shrinks under a chosen orientation of modular time.

Third, the lines

\[
u=a,\quad u=b,\quad v=a,\quad v=b
\]

are invariant because the corresponding component of \(V_A\) vanishes there. They partition kinematic space into sectors according to the endpoint ordering relative to \(a\) and \(b\). These invariant lines are the kinematic counterparts of modular horizons.

For example:

- If \(a<u<v<b\), both endpoints lie inside \(A\). Both flow toward the same future fixed endpoint \(b\), although at rates determined by their initial positions.
- If \(u<a<b<v\), the test interval contains \(A\). Its left and right endpoints lie in different sign regions of \(\zeta_A\), so the two ends move in opposite directions.
- If \(u<a<v<b\), the intervals overlap without nesting. One endpoint is outside and one inside, producing a different orbit sector.
- If both endpoints lie entirely outside on the same side, their modular trajectories remain in that exterior component.

Thus the vector field geometrizes endpoint-order relations among intervals.

## 7. The kinematic metric and entropy density

For the vacuum of a \(2\)-dimensional CFT on the line, the entropy of one interval is

\[
S(u,v)
=
\frac{c}{3}
\log\frac{v-u}{\epsilon},
\]

where \(c\) is the central charge and \(\epsilon\) is a UV cutoff. The mixed derivative is cutoff independent:

\[
\frac{\partial^2S}{\partial u\,\partial v}
=
\frac{c}{3(v-u)^2}.
\]

A common kinematic line element is therefore, up to convention-dependent normalization,

\[
ds_{\mathcal K}^{2}
=
\frac{\partial^2S}{\partial u\,\partial v}\,du\,dv
=
\frac{c}{3}\frac{du\,dv}{(v-u)^2}.
\]

Because \(du\,dv=d\theta^2-d\alpha^2\), this has Lorentzian signature:

\[
ds_{\mathcal K}^{2}
\propto
\frac{d\theta^2-d\alpha^2}{\alpha^2}.
\]

It is a planar patch of two-dimensional de Sitter geometry. The associated Crofton form is

\[
\omega
=
\frac{\partial^2S}{\partial u\,\partial v}\,du\wedge dv.
\]

Operationally, this mixed second derivative is the continuum density entering conditional mutual information for adjacent infinitesimal intervals. The integral-geometric formulation identifies the kinematic measure with conditional mutual-information density and, in holographic states obeying the Ryu–Takayanagi relation, uses it to reconstruct lengths of bulk curves ([Czech et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05515)).

The modular vector field \(V_A\) is a Killing vector of the vacuum kinematic metric. This follows because \(\zeta_A\) is one of the global conformal generators, and the metric is invariant under the diagonal Möbius action on \((u,v)\). Consequently, modular flow moves intervals along kinematic isometries: it changes their endpoints while preserving the vacuum kinematic geometry.

This does **not** mean that every interval retains the same regulated entropy under an arbitrary coordinate description. The cutoff also transforms, and entanglement entropy is regulator dependent. The geometrically cleaner invariant is the mixed derivative or Crofton form.

## 8. What the geometry reveals about entanglement

The vector-field picture reveals entanglement structure in several precise but limited senses.

### 8.1 Scale and position become geometric directions

A kinematic point records both the center and size of an interval. Modular flow therefore acts simultaneously on location and scale. In the expression

\[
V_A
=
\frac{\pi}{R}
\left[
(R^2-\theta^2-\alpha^2)\partial_\theta
-
2\theta\alpha\,\partial_\alpha
\right],
\]

the \(\partial_\theta\) term translates the interval center, while the \(\partial_\alpha\) term expands or contracts its size. The reference interval is selected as the unique fixed region.

### 8.2 Kinematic horizons encode endpoint anchoring

The invariant lines where one endpoint coincides with an endpoint of \(A\) divide interval space into orbit sectors. A flow line cannot cross one of these lines at finite modular parameter. This is the region-space counterpart of the fact that modular trajectories do not cross the boundary of the reference causal diamond.

### 8.3 Entanglement density supplies the measure

The vector field gives directions of modular motion, whereas

\[
\partial_u\partial_vS
\]

weights areas in interval space. The pair \((V_A,\omega)\) therefore combines dynamics with information density: \(V_A\) identifies how regions are rearranged, and \(\omega\) identifies how strongly nearby endpoint deformations contribute to conditional mutual information.

### 8.4 The modular Hamiltonian is itself a kinematic field

Stress-tensor OPE blocks behave as fields on kinematic space. In particular, the vacuum modular Hamiltonian satisfies a Klein–Gordon-type equation determined by the conformal Casimir. For the full two-dimensional kinematic space, Czech and collaborators write

\[
\left[
2\left(\Box_{\mathrm{dS}_2}
+
\Box_{\overline{\mathrm{dS}}_2}\right)
+4
\right]H_{\mathrm{mod}}=0
\]

in their conventions ([Czech et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03110)). On a fixed-time diagonal slice this reduces to an equation on a single de Sitter space. Combined with the entanglement first law,

\[
\delta S_A=\delta\langle K_A\rangle,
\]

small entropy variations may consequently be treated as kinematic fields obeying symmetry-controlled equations.

### 8.5 Parallel transport exposes curvature

A family of regions comes with a family of modular Hamiltonians. Comparing their modular frames requires handling zero-mode ambiguities: operators commuting with \(K_A\) can change the frame without changing the modular spectrum. The modular Berry connection organizes this comparison. In the vacuum of a \(2\)-dimensional CFT, global conformal symmetry selects a natural connection, and certain Wilson loops reproduce differential-entropy expressions for bulk curve lengths ([Czech et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07123)). This is stronger than merely assigning one vector field to one region: it uses variations across kinematic space to diagnose holonomy and curvature.

## 9. Holographic interpretation—and its boundary

In a holographic \(\mathrm{CFT}_2\), the interval \((u,v)\) labels a bulk geodesic. Kinematic modular flow therefore induces a motion in the space of geodesics. The geodesic anchored to the reference interval is fixed. Other geodesics are moved by the bulk isometry extending the boundary conformal transformation.

OPE blocks have a complementary interpretation. At leading order in the large-\(N\), semiclassical regime, an OPE block is dual to an integral of a bulk field along the corresponding geodesic,

\[
c_\Delta\,\mathcal B_\Delta(u,v)
=
\int_{\gamma_{uv}}ds\,\phi_\Delta,
\]

as derived in the stereoscopic kinematic dictionary ([Czech et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.03110)). The stress-tensor block associated with the modular Hamiltonian is related to fluctuations of the extremal-surface area.

These statements should not be conflated:

1. Kinematic space as a space of intervals is meaningful in a CFT without assuming holography.
2. Its entropy-derived metric can also be defined field-theoretically.
3. Identifying every point with a classical bulk geodesic and every kinematic volume with a bulk geometric quantity additionally assumes an appropriate semiclassical holographic dual.
4. Quantum, finite-\(N\), and non-geometric states may not admit the same simple bulk interpretation.

## 10. Limitations and open questions

The explicit vector field above relies on four strong conditions: a vacuum state, conformal symmetry, a single interval or ball, and a region whose modular Hamiltonian is geometrically generated. Relaxing any of them can make modular evolution nonlocal.

Thermal states and certain conformally related states still permit controlled constructions, but the kinematic metric and modular generator change. Multiple disjoint intervals are substantially harder: their modular Hamiltonians generally contain nonlocal couplings, so endpoint motion alone cannot capture the full flow. In massive theories, conformal maps no longer determine the answer. In gauge theories, Hilbert-space factorization and edge degrees of freedom require additional choices.

Nor should kinematic modular flow be described uncritically as “information physically propagating through kinematic space.” Kinematic space is an auxiliary space of regions. A trajectory in it is a family of subregions related by modular automorphisms, not a worldline of a signal or particle. Its causal structure expresses nesting and endpoint relations; it does not automatically reproduce operational signal causality in the original spacetime.

Finally, entropy captures only part of a reduced state. Distinct density matrices can have identical von Neumann entropy, and an entropy-derived metric need not determine the modular operator. Modular spectra, zero modes, correlation functions, and Berry holonomies contain additional information. The proposal that a complete effective quantum theory lives on kinematic space remains suggestive rather than established; even work finding Virasoro-like modular eigenmodes presents that broader interpretation as a possibility, not a theorem ([Das et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10211)).

## 11. Exercises with solutions

### Exercise 1: causal order is not distance

Suppose two Lorentzian metrics satisfy \(g'_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^2(x)g_{\mu\nu}\) with positive, nonconstant \(\Omega\). Can causal order determine which metric assigns the larger proper time to a timelike curve?

**Solution.** No. The conformal rescaling preserves null cones and causal comparability, but changes proper time by \(d\tau'=\Omega(x)d\tau\). This is the required negative control: inferring metric distance from causal order alone is invalid.

### Exercise 2: identify a causal diamond

For \(A=(-R,R)\) at \(t=0\), show that the diamond is

\[
|t|+|x|<R.
\]

**Solution.** The future-directed null rays from the endpoints and the past-directed null rays from the tips have slopes \(\pm1\). Their intersection gives \(-R+|t|<x<R-|t|\), equivalent to \(|t|+|x|<R\).

### Exercise 3: construct a kinematic point

Represent the interval \((u,v)=(-2,4)\) in \((\theta,\alpha)\) coordinates.

**Solution.**

\[
\theta=\frac{-2+4}{2}=1,\qquad
\alpha=\frac{4-(-2)}{2}=3.
\]

The point \((1,3)\) in kinematic space represents the entire interval, not an event at \(x=1\).

### Exercise 4: test nesting and kinematic causality

Compare \(I_1=(-1,1)\) and \(I_2=(-2,3)\).

**Solution.** Since \(-2<-1\) and \(1<3\), \(I_1\subset I_2\). They are causally comparable in the containment order and timelike related in the standard de Sitter kinematic geometry. This does not determine their kinematic proper-time separation without the metric normalization.

### Exercise 5: evaluate the modular vector field

Take \(A=(-1,1)\) and \(I=(-1/2,1/2)\). Ignoring the common factor \(\pi\), determine the signs of \(\dot u\) and \(\dot v\).

**Solution.**

\[
R^2-u^2=1-\frac14=\frac34>0,
\qquad
R^2-v^2=\frac34>0.
\]

Both endpoints move toward the right fixed point under increasing modular parameter in the chosen convention.

### Exercise 6: connect entropy and kinematic geometry

Starting from

\[
S(u,v)=\frac{c}{3}\log\frac{v-u}{\epsilon},
\]

compute \(\partial_u\partial_v S\).

**Solution.**

\[
\partial_vS=\frac{c}{3(v-u)},
\qquad
\partial_u\partial_vS=\frac{c}{3(v-u)^2}.
\]

This positive density supplies the vacuum kinematic measure and the infinitesimal conditional-mutual-information density. It does not, by itself, reconstruct the full reduced density matrix.

## 12. Conclusion

For a vacuum interval \(A=(a,b)\) in a two-dimensional CFT, modular flow is encoded on kinematic space by

\[
V_A
=
2\pi\frac{(u-a)(b-u)}{b-a}\,\partial_u
+
2\pi\frac{(v-a)(b-v)}{b-a}\,\partial_v.
\]

The construction lifts the conformal flow of ordinary points to a diagonal flow of interval endpoints. Its fixed point is the reference interval; its invariant null-like boundaries consist of intervals sharing an endpoint with \(A\); and its orbit sectors record nesting, overlap, and exterior relations. When combined with the entropy-derived de Sitter metric and Crofton form, it relates modular dynamics to conditional mutual information, OPE-block propagation, and—under holographic assumptions—the geometry of bulk geodesics and extremal surfaces.

The established result is therefore not that entanglement literally travels through an auxiliary spacetime. It is that, in symmetry-controlled cases, modular automorphisms admit an exact geometric representation on a Lorentzian space of regions, and that this geometry organizes several distinct layers of quantum information. Extending that representation beyond vacuum balls and intervals remains a substantive open problem.
