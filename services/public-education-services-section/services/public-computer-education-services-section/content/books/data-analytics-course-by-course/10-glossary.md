# Glossary {.unnumbered}

The chapter in brackets is where each term is taught.

**Absolute reference.** A cell reference with `$` that doesn't change when
copied, such as `$C$1`. [2]

**Aggregate function.** A function that summarizes many rows: COUNT, SUM,
AVG, MIN, MAX. [5]

**Alt text.** A text description of an image or chart for people who can't
see it. [6]

**Analysis process.** Ask, prepare, process, analyze, share, act. [1]

**Bias.** A systematic distortion in data or in how it's read: sampling,
observer, interpretation, confirmation. [3]

**Changelog.** A record of every change made to data: what, why, when, how
many rows. [4]

**Common table expression (CTE).** A named intermediate result, written with
`WITH`. [5]

**Confidence level; margin of error.** How often a method captures the true
value; how far the estimate may be from it. [4]

**Credibility.** Whether a data source can be trusted: who collected it, how,
whether it's original, complete, current and cited. [3]

**CRISP-DM.** A six-phase process model for data mining. [1]

**Dashboard.** A live display of a few measures to monitor at a glance. [2,
6]

**Data ecosystem.** Everything involved in producing, storing, analyzing and
sharing data. [1]

**Data integrity.** Data's accuracy, completeness, consistency and
trustworthiness. [4]

**Data life cycle.** Plan, capture, manage, analyze, archive, destroy. [1]

**DataFrame.** A pandas table with named columns. [7]

**Dimension; measure.** In Tableau, a category; a number to aggregate. [6]

**Dirty data.** Duplicate, outdated, incomplete, incorrect or inconsistent
data. [4]

**First-, second-, third-party data.** Collected by you; by a partner who
shares it; by an outside provider. [3]

**Foreign key; primary key.** A column referring to another table's key; a
column that uniquely identifies each row. [3]

**Join.** Combining rows from two tables on a shared key: INNER, LEFT, RIGHT,
FULL OUTER. [5]

**Jupyter notebook.** A document of runnable code cells and text cells. [7]

**Long and wide data.** One observation per row; a variable's values spread
across columns. [3]

**Metadata.** Data about data: meaning, units, source, history. [3]

**NULL (NaN).** A missing, unknown value; not zero. [4, 7]

**Pivot table.** A spreadsheet tool that groups and summarizes without
formulas. [5]

**Power (statistical).** The chance a test detects a real effect of a given
size. [4]

**Proxy.** A related measure used in place of missing data. [4]

**Qualitative; quantitative data.** Described in words or categories;
measured in numbers. [2, 3]

**Scales of measurement.** Nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio. [3]

**Simpson's paradox.** A pattern within groups that reverses when the groups
are pooled. [5]

**SQL.** Structured Query Language, for querying relational databases. [1, 3]

**Structured thinking.** Defining a problem, its scope and its gaps before
analysis. [2]

**Subquery.** A query inside another query. [5]

**Tidy data.** Each variable a column, each observation a row, each kind of
unit a table. [3]

**Vectorization.** Applying an operation to a whole array at once. [7]

**VLOOKUP; XLOOKUP.** Spreadsheet functions that look up a value in one
column and return a value from another. [5]
