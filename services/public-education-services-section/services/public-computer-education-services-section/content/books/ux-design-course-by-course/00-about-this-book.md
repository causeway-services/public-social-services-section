# About this book {.unnumbered}

This book is a study companion for people taking Google's UX Design
Professional Certificate on Coursera. It follows the certificate course by
course. For each course it states what you should be able to do when you
finish, teaches the methods through one running design project, and then asks
you to recall them before it shows you the answers.

It's for you if you're taking the certificate, deciding whether to, or
finished it and want a compact review before building your portfolio or
interviewing. It assumes no design background.

## An independent companion

**This book is independent. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or
sponsored by Google, Coursera or Figma.** "Google" is a trademark of Google
LLC, "Coursera" of Coursera, Inc., and "Figma" of Figma, Inc. The book names
them only to say which credential and tool it's a companion to.

It doesn't reproduce the course's videos, readings, quizzes, templates or
graded projects, and it can't stand in for them. You earn the certificate by
completing the courses and their portfolio projects. The book teaches the same
subject from the research and standards the field is built on, and asks its
own questions.

seven-day trial [@google-ux-2026]; check the current price before you enroll.

## The running example

> **The Northside Tool Library.** A neighborhood nonprofit lends tools
> (drills, ladders, pressure washers, a tile saw) to members for a small
> yearly fee. Today members phone during two weekday evenings to ask what's
> available, and half the calls end with "it's out." The volunteers want an
> app where members can see what's in, reserve it, and get a reminder to bring
> it back. The members range from students to retirees, and some use
> screen readers or speak Spanish at home.

Each chapter takes this project one step further, from interviewing members to
a responsive website and a finished case study.

## How to use this book

Two findings from learning research shape the book. Recalling something from
memory strengthens it more than rereading it [@roediger2006]. Reviews spread
over time beat the same reviews crammed together, and the best gap grows with
how long you need to remember [@cepeda2006]. A large review of study
techniques rated these two, practice testing and spaced practice, the most
useful of the ten it examined, and rated rereading and highlighting among the
least [@dunlosky2013].

Each chapter opens with **What you will be able to do** and ends with **Check
yourself** questions and their **Answers**. Answer on paper before you look,
then come back to the same questions a few days later and again after a week
or two; the study plan lays out a schedule. Design is learned by doing: when
a chapter shows an artifact for the tool library, make the same artifact for
your own portfolio project.

## What this book doesn't do

- It doesn't grant, or count toward, the Google certificate.
- It doesn't teach the design tools step by step; the courses do that
  (in Figma, though older course materials refer to Adobe XD), and the
  tools change often. It tells you what each technique is for.
- It doesn't give you the course's portfolio prompts or templates.
- It reflects the courses as described on 27 September 2026. If a course has
  changed, trust the course.
