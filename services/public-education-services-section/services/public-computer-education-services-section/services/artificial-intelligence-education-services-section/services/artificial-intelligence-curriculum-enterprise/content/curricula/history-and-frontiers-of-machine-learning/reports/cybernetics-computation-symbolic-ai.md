# Cybernetics, computation, and symbolic AI

## Historical question

Which distinct research programs were later gathered into a history of
artificial intelligence, and what did their original sources actually claim?

## Four programs, not one inevitable lineage

Norbert Wiener's *Cybernetics* organized control and communication across
animals and machines around feedback, regulation, information, and purposive
behavior. Its cross-disciplinary ambition shaped postwar vocabulary, but it
was not simply an early name for today's machine learning.

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts studied idealized all-or-none neural units.
Their formal networks connected logical propositions with simplified neural
activity and showed how combinations of threshold-like elements could realize
logical operations. The result is historically important to neural computation
while remaining radically abstracted from biological neurons, learning data,
and modern optimization.

Alan Turing's 1950 paper proposed the imitation game as a way to replace an
ill-posed verbal dispute over whether machines think. It examined objections
and discussed learning machines. The paper did not establish a universal
intelligence metric, nor did it define every later use of the phrase “Turing
test.” Its operational reframing and its speculative learning discussion should
be kept distinct.

Allen Newell, J. C. Shaw, and Herbert Simon described the Logic Theory Machine
as a symbolic information-processing system that used heuristic search to find
proofs. The program demonstrated that a digital system could manipulate formal
expressions and discover proofs in a bounded domain. Its success neither made
general intelligence synonymous with theorem proving nor rendered neural or
cybernetic approaches obsolete.

## Relational map

These programs overlapped through people, institutions, metaphors, and
computing machinery, but their primitive objects differed:

- cybernetics foregrounded feedback, communication, and control;
- formal neuron models foregrounded networks of idealized switching units;
- Turing foregrounded computability, behavioral inquiry, and learning-machine
  possibilities;
- early symbolic AI foregrounded explicit symbols, heuristics, search, and
  formal problem spaces.

The useful history is therefore a map of partially interacting programs, not a
single staircase leading naturally to contemporary deep learning.

## Scope limits and negative controls

- The later labels “AI,” “neural network,” and “symbolic AI” are used here as
  retrospective organizers; the sources' own terminology remains primary.
- The four sources do not exhaust early machine intelligence, statistics,
  operations research, information theory, or relevant non-US traditions.
- A formal model's representational result is not evidence of biological
  fidelity or practical trainability.
- Invalid inference: “Turing proposed an imitation game, therefore any modern
  conversational benchmark measures general intelligence.” The task,
  population, evaluator, and inference target still require validation.
- Invalid inference: “the Logic Theory Machine proved theorems, therefore
  symbolic reasoning had solved intelligence.” It solved selected formal tasks
  using a particular representation and heuristic process.

## Explainer direction

Build four simultaneous timelines rather than one. Give each program its own
primitive objects—feedback loops, threshold units, conversational transcripts,
and symbolic proof trees. Let institutional and conceptual bridges appear only
where the packet warrants them. End by asking which primitives persist inside
modern systems and which historical differences current terminology hides.
