## The offer

**Multi-Agent Operating Discipline for Teams** — a live, synchronous
cohort for people who already run more than one AI coding agent and are
tired of finding out the hard way what breaks: redone work, silently
overwritten files, a "done" claim that wasn't true on disk.

This is not a general "learn to use Claude Code" course. It teaches one
narrow, tested discipline: how to write agent briefs that don't produce
confidently wrong work, how to tell a genuine parallel-work split from a
fan-out anti-pattern that wastes tokens, how to steer and recover an
agent mid-flight, and how to use dated receipts so a team's agent
sessions don't have to re-derive the same ground truth every day. The
final session is not a hypothetical — it walks a real, dated incident
where two concurrent agent sessions collided on one shared, uncommitted
checkout, and shows exactly how it got resolved.

- **Format:** live, synchronous, video call. Five sessions (four at 90
  minutes, the closing case-study session at 120 minutes).
- **Seats:** 10-20.
- **Price:** $349 per seat (see pricing rationale below).
- **Audience:** engineering leads, solo technical founders, and
  developers already paying for Claude Code, Codex, or a comparable
  agentic coding subscription who run more than one agent at a time.
- **Prerequisite:** an active agentic-coding subscription and at least
  one real backlog item to work from — every session includes a live
  exercise against something the participant actually owns.

### Session outline

1. **The Complete Brief** — what a subagent with no memory of your
   conversation must be told before you dispatch it.
2. **The Fan-Out Anti-Pattern** — when splitting work across agents
   changes the answer, and when it just burns tokens.
3. **Steering, Interrupting, and Recovering a Running Agent** — how to
   correct a running agent mid-flight and pick a dropped commission back
   up without redoing finished work.
4. **Receipts and Lifecycle Journaling as Inter-Session Memory** — how a
   dated receipt lets tomorrow's session (or a teammate) skip
   re-deriving what today's already established.
5. **Case Study: From Collision to Coordination** — a real, dated
   incident (2026-08-31: two concurrent agent sessions sharing one
   uncommitted checkout, a collision, and how it was resolved by direct
   message and a path-manifest custody split) walked start to finish, with
   a live coordination-protocol exercise.

The full session-by-session syllabus, learning objectives, and
facilitation plans are the structured course carrier at
`content/curricula/multi-agent-operating-discipline-for-teams.v1.json`,
with one detailed lesson brief per session under
`content/lesson-briefs/multi-agent-operating-discipline-for-teams/`.

## Pricing rationale

The org's segment theory (`align-to-roadmap` SKILL.md, "The foundry
presumption") holds that the founder is the org's own proven demand: a
solo operator paying flat-rate for agentic coding tools monthly, and the
segment the founder represents pays real subscription-sized dollars for
exactly this kind of tooling literacy. This cohort prices into that same
segment, deliberately below the full-bootcamp comparables researched
below, because it teaches one narrow discipline in five sessions rather
than a multi-week platform bootcamp.

Real, cited comparables found via WebSearch/WebFetch on 2026-08-31:

- **Claude Code in Practice** (Hamza Farooq & Aishwarya Ashok, Maven):
  $2,000, 10 live sessions over roughly four weeks (Sept 2 - Oct 4,
  2026). <https://maven.com/boring-bot/claude-code-in-practice>
- **Agentic AI for Claude Builders** (James Gray, Maven): $1,500, 5
  weeks, live sessions twice weekly, four prior cohorts run at 4.8/5.
  <https://maven.com/james-gray/claude>
- **An 8-week applied-LLM Maven cohort** (Hamel Husain, cited via a
  prompt-engineering course roundup): $750-$1,500.
  <https://pecollective.com/blog/best-prompt-engineering-courses/>
- **The AI Engineer Certification** (AI Makerspace, Maven): $4,000,
  Sept 22 - Dec 7, 2026, capped at 80 students, a much longer
  full-bootcamp program. <https://aimakerspace.io/the-ai-engineer-certification/>
- **Harness Engineering Workshop** (Vizuara): ₹60,000 early-bird
  (approximately $700 at the time researched), a 5-day live cohort
  building a coding-agent harness from scratch.
  <https://harnessengineering.vizuara.ai/>

Every one of these comparables is a multi-week program (4-10 live
sessions, 5-10 weeks) covering the breadth of agentic tool usage.
**Multi-Agent Operating Discipline for Teams** is deliberately narrower
and shorter — five sessions, one discipline, real incident material —
and is priced accordingly: **$349 per seat**, inside the $200-500 brief
range and well under every comparable's per-session cost (each
comparable runs $150-$400 per session; this cohort runs five sessions
for $349 total). At 15 seats (the midpoint of 10-20), a sold-out cohort
grosses $5,235 — a plausible, unembarrassing first-cohort number for an
unproven live-teaching brand, not a number this session invented without
comparison.

No price point above is this session's invention; each traces to the
cited URL. The $349 figure itself is this session's own estimate,
positioned deliberately below the cited comparables' per-session cost
given the shorter format and first-cohort (unproven-brand) status.

### Named rails vs. x402

**Decision (this session): named-rails checkout (a conventional
processor or a cohort-hosting platform's built-in checkout, such as
Maven's), not the org's x402/pseudonymous rails.** A synchronous paid
cohort with 10-20 real human seats needs real buyer identity (who is on
the call), refund handling, and support contact — none of which x402's
agent-to-agent micropayment design is built for. x402 is the right rail
for machine-to-machine microcents (the org's other live lane); a live
class with named humans attending video calls is squarely the
conventional-commerce shape instead. This is stated as a decision, not a
question, per the founder's standing engineering-judgment authorization.
