# How do climate hazards become unequal housing costs through maps, insurance, and mortgages?

## Claims and uncertainty

- A flood map is an institutional model used for decisions, not the hazard itself: different datasets and methods can classify the same housing market differently and can omit material inland or forward-looking exposure.
  - Warrants: cfpb-2025-flood-risk-mortgage-market, fhfa-2024-climate-risk-exposure
  - Uncertainty: The CFPB comparison covers selected U.S. census regions and one private model alongside FEMA data; neither model is a complete or certain prediction of property-level losses.
- Insurance transmits physical hazard into recurring household cost and housing-market access: recent premium growth was much larger in some disaster-prone places, while reduced coverage can put downward pressure on property values and increase mortgage losses.
  - Warrants: gao-2026-homeowners-insurance, fhfa-2024-climate-risk-exposure
  - Uncertainty: Premium changes vary by peril, property, insurer, state regulation, rebuilding cost and time period; associations between modeled risk and financial outcomes do not establish one inevitable result for every home.
- The boundary between mapped and unmapped risk can distribute protection unequally because mortgage and insurance practices respond to classifications, while households exposed to underrecognized inland flood risk may have fewer resources to absorb an uninsured loss.
  - Warrants: cfpb-2025-flood-risk-mortgage-market
  - Uncertainty: The cited income and down-payment differences describe groups in the study region and period; they should not be treated as a causal estimate or generalized to every borrower.
- Who ultimately pays for climate-related housing risk is jointly produced by hazard, modeling assumptions, insurance underwriting, state regulation, lending rules, property values and household balance sheets rather than determined by climate physics alone.
  - Warrants: gao-2026-homeowners-insurance, cfpb-2025-flood-risk-mortgage-market, fhfa-2024-climate-risk-exposure
  - Uncertainty: These sources illuminate U.S. federal housing and insurance institutions but do not fully capture renters, uninsured owners, local tax systems, private securitization or every state insurance market.

## Method

Compare three primary U.S. government sources at distinct institutional layers: observed homeowners-insurance pricing, mortgage-market exposure under competing flood-risk models, and exploratory housing-finance climate scenario analysis. Separate observed premium changes from modeled exposure, treat map boundaries as decision instruments, and trace how risk moves through contracts and regulation into household costs.

## Limitations

- The packet is U.S.-focused and does not establish how housing risk is allocated in other legal or insurance systems.
- It does not estimate the climate risk, insurance price or mortgage outcome for any specific property.
- The sources center homeowners and mortgage finance; renters, people without mortgages and unhoused people require additional evidence.
- The packet supports educational relational orientation, not insurance, legal, mortgage, investment or property-purchase advice.
