## **Discord Communities & Culture**

Since you’re **coming from IRC** and aren’t deeply familiar with Discord’s culture, let’s step back and talk about **how communities actually function** on this platform.

Discord isn’t just a chat server—it’s a **highly social, multi-channel space** with its own **norms, engagement strategies, and retention dynamics**.

---

### **🏗️ How Strong Discord Communities Are Built**

A Discord community is like a **tiny digital town**—and for it to thrive, you need more than just governance. You need **culture, engagement, and a sense of belonging**.

Here’s what **healthy, decentralized** Discord communities usually have:

### **1️⃣ A Strong "Why" (Clear Community Identity)**

People don’t just join a Discord **for governance**—they join because they resonate with its **mission and energy**.

Your **mission** is already solid:  
🌱 **Helping people move from reacting to climate weirding → to responding to it.**

But to make that **actionable**, the community needs to feel **clear and inviting**. Some key things that help:  
✔ A **simple, one-sentence pinned message** in a welcome channel that says what this place is about.  
✔ A **clear onboarding experience** that helps people find relevant spaces.  
✔ A **culture of shared action**—not just talking about climate responses, but actually **doing things together**.

---

### **2️⃣ Onboarding & Member Flow (How People Get Engaged)**

**One of the biggest mistakes** in Discords is assuming people will “figure it out” on their own. Most won’t. You need to **guide them smoothly into participation.**

Right now, people will:

1. **Join the server.**
    
2. **See a bunch of channels.**
    
3. ???
    

We don’t want that _confusion gap_. Instead, we want:  
✔ A **Welcome & Start Here channel** → Explains what the server is for in 1-2 sentences.  
✔ A **“Choose Your Interests” role system** → Lets people self-assign roles to unlock relevant channels.  
✔ A **First Easy Action** → Give new members something small & immediate to do (e.g., vote in a fun poll, introduce themselves, react to a post).

This **onboarding flow** ensures new members don’t just **lurk**—they start **participating** from day one.

---

### **3️⃣ Channel Structure & Organization (Avoiding the Mess)**

Most Discords **fail** because they become a **spaghetti mess of channels**—too many spaces, no clear structure, and people get overwhelmed.

You're already **thinking well about governance**, but here’s how to structure your **entire** community clearly:

#### **🔹 Core Sections Your Server Needs**

🟢 **Community Hub** (For Everyone)

- **#welcome** → “What this place is about”
    
- **#announcements** → Major updates
    
- **#introductions** → “Say hi & share your interests”
    

🟡 **Topical Discussion Channels** (Where action happens)

- **#climate-strategies** → Discuss real-world tactics
    
- **#resource-sharing** → Post helpful guides, links, tools
    
- **#mutual-aid** → Organizing support for people
    

🔵 **Governance Section** (For transparency & decision-making)

- **#governance-discussion** → Informal proposal talk
    
- **#governance-action** → Where final decisions get posted
    
- **#governance-feedback** → Non-Governors weigh in
    
- **#governance-questions** → Open to all for learning
    

This **keeps things structured** while making sure people **find what they need easily**.

---

### **4️⃣ Engagement & Retention (Keeping People Active)**

A Discord isn’t a **“set it and forget it”** space. You need **consistent activity** or it’ll feel **empty**, and people will **drift away**.

Here’s what strong communities **do** to stay alive:  
✔ **Regular Check-Ins** – Weekly discussion prompts, challenges, or polls.  
✔ **Recognition & Rewards** – Celebrating contributors, even just with shoutouts.  
✔ **Community Rituals** – Recurring calls, reading groups, action sprints.  
✔ **Low-Friction Engagement** – Not every action should be “big”; let people participate in **quick & easy** ways (emoji reactions, quick questions).
