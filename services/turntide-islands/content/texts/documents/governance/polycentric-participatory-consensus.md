# Polycentric participatory consensus

This document turns historical governance discussion into a protocol that can
be evaluated. It is not a constitution currently in force.

## The cycle

Turntide Islands can distinguish:

`values + position → orientation → direction → strategy and tactics → plan → action → reflection → reconsidered values`

Orientation describes where the community is and what it values. Direction
chooses where to move from that position. Strategy and tactics describe how.
Plans allocate particular work. Action changes conditions. Reflection returns
evidence to the next orientation.

Governance should not collapse every part of this cycle into a yes/no vote.
Conversation, research, experiments, administrative work, and decisions each
have different functions.

## A proposed decision lifecycle

1. **Discussion.** Establish the question, affected polycenters, evidence,
   constraints, and dissent.
2. **Proposal.** State the exact decision, responsible implementer, review
   time, success evidence, and rollback.
3. **Participation window.** Notify the affected body and preserve the ability
   to vote yes, no, or abstain.
4. **Quorum and outcome.** Determine participation separately from preference.
5. **Implementation.** A named human administrator or explicitly authorized
   automation performs only the accepted action.
6. **Receipt.** Record the proposal, tally, decision, implementation evidence,
   and unresolved objections in a durable public system.
7. **Reflection and appeal.** Review effects, correct the record, and reopen
   decisions when their stated conditions fail.

## Polycenters

Topical islands should decide matters local to their own work unless a decision
changes shared rights, infrastructure, resources, or constitutional rules.
Shared governance protects the ability of polycenters to differ while defining
the boundaries they cannot unilaterally cross.

## Historical thresholds to test

A March 2025 administrator proposal suggested:

- 50% quorum for changes to how decisions are made;
- 20% quorum for ordinary decisions;
- an active governor body whose membership expires after a month without
  participation and can be restored on request.

These numbers and the activity rule are hypotheses, not present policy. Before
ratification the community should define the eligible body, abstention,
conflicts, delegation, minimum notice, ties, failed quorum, emergencies,
accessibility, and what forms of participation count.

## Automation boundary

Zelo or another bot may publish notices, validate a proposal shape, collect
votes, calculate a transparent tally, and draft a receipt. It may not invent
community memory, select the legitimate electorate, interpret ambiguous
mandates, or expand its own authority. Configuration and releases must be
visible enough to govern, with a named human recovery path.
