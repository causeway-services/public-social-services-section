# Recognition, not permission

Turntide Islands begins from a severe premise: climate breakdown and
institutional decay are present conditions, not merely future risks. The useful
distinction is therefore response rather than reaction. Response develops the
capacity to live, care, learn, and coordinate under changing conditions;
reaction waits for the old stability to return or seeks permission from the
systems producing the crisis.

This does not yield one blueprint. Adaptation can involve food, land,
infrastructure, communication, mutual defense, teaching, or care. It can be
done by collectives or individuals. Turntide Islands' proposed role is to help
people recognize, connect, and materially support responsible work rather than
claim central authority over what all adaptation must look like.

## Recognition as a governance act

The historical `TTIDAO` thesis proposed that governance could recognize work
already being done:

- a member describes useful adaptation work or a concrete need;
- another member can vouch for it;
- the community discusses the claim and its context;
- a quorum-compatible vote can recognize the contribution or direct bounded
  support;
- the decision and implementation leave a durable receipt.

Recognition is neither permission to act nor a universal certificate of merit.
It is a situated community judgment: this work happened, matters here, and
should not go unsupported.

## Proof of adaptation

The proposed token was conceived as proof of adaptation: a non-speculative
record that people trusted a person or project because of demonstrated work.
It was not described as a commodity whose purchase grants legitimacy.

That historical concept suggests requirements for any future implementation:

- influence cannot be purchased without contribution;
- recognition needs attributable evidence and correction;
- treasury support and recognition weight remain distinguishable;
- a token cannot become a promise of profit;
- no balance substitutes for current care, accountability, or competence;
- issuance, delegation, concentration, expiry, and appeals require explicit
  governance;
- the system must remain useful without forcing members to hold a wallet.

## Polycentric application

Recognition can be local to topical islands. Gardening, knowledge rescue,
regional work, governance, and technical maintenance need not use one
centralized definition of contribution. Each polycenter can develop appropriate
evidence while sharing community-wide guarantees for discussion, quorum,
receipts, conflicts, and appeals.
